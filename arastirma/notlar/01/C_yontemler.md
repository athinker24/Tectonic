## PART 1: Scientists and geologists reconstructing tectonics for fictional worlds

### 1.1 Game of Thrones / Westeros and Essos, University of Sydney, 2019 (GPlates)

**Who**
- The Conversation article "We made a moving tectonic map of the Game of Thrones landscape" was written by Sabin Zahirovic (University of Sydney) and Jo Condon (University of Melbourne). It was published on 20 May 2019, the day of the series finale. https://www.auscope.org.au/media-1/got ; https://www.livescience.com/65525-game-of-thrones-geology-mapped.html
- Research assistants Cian Clinton-Gray, Irene Koutsoumbis and Youseph Ibrahim helped build the map and write the article. https://www.auscope.org.au/media-1/got
- The YouTube animation "Tectonic reconstruction of Westeros and Essos in GPlates" was posted on Sabin Zahirovic's channel. Its description snippet reads: "This was a demonstration project of how to build plate tectonic ...". Title and author come from YouTube oEmbed: https://www.youtube.com/oembed?url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HB_ky-EAQtU&format=json . The snippet comes from YouTube search results: https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=game+of+thrones+plate+tectonics+gplates
- **Ben Mather: UNVERIFIED** as a contributor to the GoT map. No page I read names him. He did co-run the EarthByte "GPlates and pyGPlates Workshop" (3-6 May 2022) with Zahirovic and Nicky Wright, per the YouTube search snippet for the EarthByte channel videos 0vFWpxfgAWE, 5Bf38ZSsT5A and 8y1FpB4eUII (same YouTube search URL as above).
- Wikipedia's GPlates article says that in 2019 "two Australian researchers used the software to create a tectonic map of the continents within the fictional Game of Thrones universe". It cites Condon & Zahirovic in The Conversation. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPlates

**Software**
- They used GPlates, free community software developed at the University of Sydney School of Geosciences. The authors note that the same software is used by hobbyist "planet builders" for games and films. https://www.auscope.org.au/media-1/got
- Wikipedia adds that GPlates gained a following among worldbuilders, especially after the YouTuber Artifexian used it and made tutorials. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPlates

**Base map**
- The present-day map caption reads: "Author modified, digital GIS files from cadei at www.cartographersguild.com". In other words, they started from a fan-made GIS vector map of the fixed fictional coastline. https://www.livescience.com/65525-game-of-thrones-geology-mapped.html
- Other pages credit the Westeros GIS data to "cadaei of the cartographersguild website forum" (SNIPPET): https://plotnine.org/gallery/territories-of-westeros.html

**Method and clues used (from the authors' own text)**
- "we use the shapes of continents and the geology they carry to reconstruct how real tectonic plate 'puzzle pieces' moved around". https://www.auscope.org.au/media-1/got
- They used "evidence" collected by themselves and others from the fictional world, "including evidence of past volcanism and mountain building, which are often the smoking gun for tectonic plate convergence and collision". https://www.auscope.org.au/media-1/got
- The present-day map shows red sawtooth lines for subduction zones "where tectonic plates are converging, leading to mountain building and volcanism (like the Andes)". https://www.livescience.com/65525-game-of-thrones-geology-mapped.html
- Geological clues cited from the story: snow-capped volcanic peaks north of the Wall; Dragonglass (obsidian) and Valyrian steel from the volcanic cliffs around Dragonstone; the contrasting topography of the Dothraki steppe. https://www.auscope.org.au/media-1/got
- Explicit inference rule: "The easiest part of the tectonic reconstruction takes place by working backwards from seafloor spreading". In other words, close the oceans first. https://www.auscope.org.au/media-1/got
- Key assumption: Westeros and Essos broke apart 25 Ma to open the Narrow Sea, "much like the unzipping of the African continent along the East African Rift Valley at a similar time". The analogue is real Earth tectonics of the same age. https://www.auscope.org.au/media-1/got
- Futura-Sciences (French) adds two details: a "supercontinent" formed about 50 Ma before the 25 Ma split, and the simulation covers almost 600 Myr. https://www.futura-sciences.com/planete/breves/geologie-video-tectonique-plaques-game-thrones-709/ . The 50 Ma supercontinent appears only in this secondary source.

**Acknowledged limits and liberties**
- Deeper in time, evidence is lost to erosion, to continental collisions that build mountains, and to subduction. The authors give India/Tethys/Tibet as an example of evidence being crushed. https://www.auscope.org.au/media-1/got
- "reconstructions back to the Pangea supercontinent at 250 million years ago are fairly accurate by just undoing seafloor spreading, but the restoration of older supercontinents are much more difficult". https://www.auscope.org.au/media-1/got
- The 25 Ma split is described as an assumption ("we've assumed"). https://www.auscope.org.au/media-1/got
- The animation first shows the Westeros/Essos model, then real Earth. https://www.auscope.org.au/media-1/got
- Secondary coverage repeats the original article: https://www.inverse.com/article/55992-moving-game-of-thrones-map-geology-tectonic-plates-continents-westeros-essos ; https://www.kqed.org/science/1941980/heres-that-game-of-thrones-plate-tectonics-animation-youve-been-waiting-for ; https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/game-of-thrones-tectonic-map ; https://winteriscoming.net/2021/08/23/scientists-create-tectonic-map-world-ice-fire-westeros-essos ; https://wikiofthrones.com/33586/a-scientifically-accurate-tectonic-map-of-the-world-of-game-of-thrones-created-by-scientists-at-the-university-of-sydney/
- The authors published no explicit inference-rule list, plate count, or GPlates files. I found no download on EarthByte (search only).

### 1.2 Stanford "Generation Anthropocene": geologic history of Westeros, April 2014

- Miles Traer and colleagues pieced the history together "from character observations, town names, official Game of Thrones maps, and the principles of geology learned here on Earth". From this "limited data" they reimagined 500 Myr of evolution: volcanoes, continents rising from the oceans, and ice ages. https://news.yahoo.com/stanford-researchers-map-world-game-thrones-014537545.html
- Example inference: the Mountains of the Moon rose 80-100 Ma as oceanic crust between northern and southern Westeros subducted under the south. The Lannister gold is explained by analogy with the California Gold Country. https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/love-game-thrones-now-you-can-learn-about-westeros-geology-stanford-1444718
- They estimated planet size from the Wall-to-Dorne distance (3,000 miles), giving a radius of 4,297 miles. https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/love-game-thrones-now-you-can-learn-about-westeros-geology-stanford-1444718
- Reactor (Tor) summarises it: "figure out where the tectonic plates are, find missing volcanoes"; Westeros evolved "from southern hemispheric lump to its jagged current-day self". https://reactormag.com/geological-history-of-westeros/
- Commenter critiques, useful as a list of pitfalls: no triple junction evident at the Westeros-Essos ridge; reliance on mid-Atlantic spreading rates; red mountains compared to the Appalachians but explained as subduction-only; "this only really works on the level of jigsaw-puzzle continents and guessing collisions from mountain ranges". https://reactormag.com/geological-history-of-westeros/
- Gizmodo's timeline summary (SNIPPET): Black Mountains 60-80 Ma, Essos separating 25 Ma. https://gizmodo.com/a-fantastically-detailed-geological-history-for-game-of-1561092800 (page did not load)
- Miles Traer also applied geology to the Doom of Valyria: chained eruptions in the Fourteen Flames plus a meteor impact. https://www.motionpictures.org/?p=9466

### 1.3 Middle-earth: geologists' plate reconstructions (Howes 1967, Reynolds 1974, Sarjeant 1990s)

- Howes (1967), Reynolds (1974) and Sarjeant (1992/1995) used the illustrations, maps and text to build conjectural geologies. Howes correlated the Elder Days with Pleistocene glaciations. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Middle-earth
- **Reynolds 1974 (four plates):** Rhovanion (north), Eriador (west), Harad and Mordor (south). The Anduin runs in an aulacogen (failed rift); there is a tectonic basin between the White Mountains and Emyn Muil; Rohan is a craton. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Middle-earth
- **Sarjeant (six plates):**
  - Eriador and Forlindon collided to build the Ered Luin, with Forlindon mostly subducted.
  - Eriador collided with Rhovanion and Harad to make the Misty and White Mountains, which meet beside the stable Rohan craton.
  - A collision with the Forodwaith plate raised the Ered Mithrin. Gundabad is a tough block at a triple junction.
  - The Mordor plate collided with Harad and Rhovanion, tearing the Anduin rift, which is subsiding.
  - Mount Doom, Dol Guldur, Orthanc and Erebor are isolated volcanoes. The Moria West-gate is a possible sill.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Middle-earth
- Notable inference style: **mountain ranges are read as sutures, junctions of ranges are read as triple junctions, a long straight river is read as a rift, and flat stable plains are read as cratons.** Sarjeant's map does not mark the boundaries; "you can assume they lie near the mountain ranges". https://oliviarafferty.substack.com/p/the-geology-of-middle-earth
- Hazel Gibson (EGU GeoLog, 2023): Sarjeant had to make "some very large leaps", especially over the Anduin. Near right-angle range junctions are possible but "you have to work really hard to find them". Mordor, a "square plain fenced by mountains at right angles", is "practically impossible". Tolkien's own canon explains it as magic (Morgoth reshaping the land). https://blogs.egu.eu/geolog/2023/09/29/far-over-the-misty-mountains-cold-to-dungeons-deep-and-caverns-old-the-geology-of-the-lord-of-the-rings/
- Tolkien said the map "was devised 'dramatically' rather than geologically" (quoted by Rafferty). https://oliviarafferty.substack.com/p/the-geology-of-middle-earth
- Rafferty gives the Tarim Basin as a real analogue for Mordor: a flat desert ringed by mountains. https://oliviarafferty.substack.com/p/the-geology-of-middle-earth

### 1.4 Middle-earth: geologist critiques and rebuttals (Acks 2017, Mapsburgh 2017, EGU GD 2025)

**Alex Acks (geologist), Tor.com 2017, mountains article**
- "mountain ranges are the fingerprints left behind by tectonic activity". Without tectonics, land erodes back to base level.
- The three ways to make mountains are: ocean-under-continent subduction (Pacific Rim), continent-continent collision (Himalaya), and extension (horst and graben: Basin and Range, Baikal, East Africa).
- Interior ranges mark sutures.
- Acks accepts the west-coast ranges as an active margin and the Misty Mountains as a collision. Acks rejects the near-perpendicular ranges: "Mountains don't do corners." The box of Mordor is rejected outright.
- An isolated volcano like Mount Doom requires a hotspot, and hotspots make age-progressive chains. Hotspots are mostly oceanic; on continents they give dike swarms or caldera chains (Yellowstone).
- Acks says fantasy maps drawn after the 1960s get no pass on this.
https://reactormag.com/tolkiens-map-and-the-messed-up-mountains-of-middle-earth/

**Acks, rivers article**
- "Water flows downhill." The drainage basin is the fundamental unit, and landscapes without branching rivers and clear divides are called "deranged".
- A river crossing a range is acceptable if the river is antecedent (older than the range, like the Colorado River through Basin and Range).
- Missing tributaries may be a map-resolution issue, but a one-sided tributary pattern implies the river is not at the low point.
- An inland delta (the Entwash) requires baselevel, so it makes no sense if the main river keeps flowing past it.
- Lakes and inland seas are local baselevels and need watersheds.
- Stream networks are fractal, which is "the number one way to artificially generate a realistic-looking drainage pattern".
https://reactormag.com/tolkiens-map-and-the-perplexing-river-systems-of-middle-earth/

**Mapsburgh, "In Defense of Tolkien's Mountains" (2017)**
This is a worked example of inferring a history from a fixed map.
- The ranges are not actually straight: the Misty Mountains form an S-curve and the White Mountains an arc. The map may be a stylised in-world artefact, like medieval maps.
- Misty Mountains: a recent or active continental collision between Eriador and Rhovanion, "more like the Himalayas than the Urals".
- Rhovanion uplands: a Deccan-like flood basalt. Erebor is the end of a hotspot chain, with the plate moving west.
- The Anduin runs parallel to the range like the Ganges, blocked by a volcanic plateau.
- Blue Mountains: not subduction, because there is no continuation along the coast and no offshore arc, and the range is short and broken. Instead it is an old collision with Beleriand, followed by rifting that opened Belegaer. This is an Appalachian-style Wilson cycle.
- South: Harad moves north. Subduction under Belfalas makes the White Mountains, Gondor is a collage of accreted terranes, and Mordor is a microplate squeezed between plates (an Anatolia/Arabia analogue).
- The output is a map with convergent boundaries in red, divergent in blue, and green motion arrows.
https://mapsburgh.tumblr.com/post/163719865416/in-defense-of-tolkiens-mountains

**EGU Geodynamics blog, 13 Aug 2025, "One ring to rule them all: the geology of Middle-earth"**
(The author shown on the page appears to be Frank Zwaan, but this is UNVERIFIED as authorship and not just a page credit.)
- Misty Mountains: a continent-continent collision belt. The clues are linear elongation (suture), high rugged relief (young orogen), mithril (hydrothermal/magmatic), and the Balrog's domain (post-orogenic magmatism).
- Mordor: a subduction arc, a rift with a graben, or a caldera.
- White Mountains: an older, eroded orogen (Variscan/Appalachian analogue). The clues are karst, plateaus and relict peaks.
- Clue-to-age logic: young orogens are high and rugged; old ones are broad, eroded and karstic.
https://blogs.egu.eu/divisions/gd/2025/08/13/one-ring-to-rule-them-all-the-geology-of-middle-earth/

**Exilian (Jubal)**
- Most of Tolkien's ranges "fit together well in naturally flowing lines and curves". The Ered Luin is a candidate for a current plate boundary.
- Mordor's inward drainage correctly forms the Sea of Nurnen.
https://exilian.co.uk/article.php?id=116180

### 1.5 Lunt, "The Climate of Middle Earth" (as "Radagast the Brown", Bristol, Dec 2013)

This is the climate counterpart: a fixed fictional map turned into model boundary conditions.
- Model: HadCM3L (UK Met Office GCM), with three runs (Modern Earth, Late Cretaceous "Dinosaur Earth", Middle Earth). https://www.paleo.bristol.ac.uk/~ggdjl/reports/middle_earth_1.4.pdf
- The boundary conditions were taken from Tolkien's maps and Fonstad's Atlas, using a Second Age Northern Hemisphere map of Arda. https://www.paleo.bristol.ac.uk/~ggdjl/reports/middle_earth_1.4.pdf
- **Explicit rules for turning a drawn map into heights** (useful as a baseline): foothills = 2000 m, mountains = 4000 m, rivers = 100 m, all other land = 300 m, plus a small random value per gridbox for undrawn features. Ocean depth increases progressively away from the coast to a maximum of 4000 m. https://www.paleo.bristol.ac.uk/~ggdjl/reports/middle_earth_1.4.pdf
- The circular flat world was mapped directly onto a lat/lon grid with missing corners set to ocean, at 0.5 deg resolution. Initial vegetation was shrubs everywhere. https://www.paleo.bristol.ac.uk/~ggdjl/reports/middle_earth_1.4.pdf
- Acknowledged liberties:
  - The map was not traced "fastidiously" ("I had misplaced my spectacles").
  - The mapping onto a sphere is uncertain, and "other equally-valid, or better solutions could be found".
  - Second Age and Third Age climates are assumed similar.
  - The run lasted only 70 years, so it is far from equilibrium.
  - CO2 was 4x preindustrial after year 50.
  - Same radius, spin rate and spin direction as Earth.
  https://www.paleo.bristol.ac.uk/~ggdjl/reports/middle_earth_1.4.pdf
- Results: the Misty Mountains create a rain shadow; Mordor is like LA or west Texas; the Shire is like Leicestershire or Belarus. The model ran about 5 days of compute for 70 model-years. https://www.pbs.org/newshour/science/modeling-middle-earths-climate-isnt-magic-its-science

## PART 2: Worldbuilding community methods for an already-drawn map

### 2.1 Worldbuilding Pasta (WBP, "An Apple Pie From Scratch" series)

**Position on retrofitting (key)**
- The author does not recommend importing a tectonic history onto an existing map. https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html (comment thread)

**Recipe for an existing map** (author's own reply in the comments)
1. Check whether the current continents "might reasonably have originated from a single supercontinent". It is fine to leave one or two landmasses out.
2. That gives current plate directions, "generally away from the center of the former supercontinent", although plates can change direction quickly when new subduction starts.
3. From the directions, predict current plate boundaries and ongoing orogenies.
4. "Then it's basically just an artistic choice where to place older mountains and features."

https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html

**Feasibility test** (author's reply)
- The key question is whether you can trace things back to the breakup of the last supercontinent without "random new landmasses popping up in the middle of the ocean far from any subduction zone, or the lack of any leading-edge active margins on your continents".
- After that, you have "cleaned the slate" and can extrapolate back as far as you like.

https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html

**Why it is hard** (author's reply in the GPlates supplement)
- It is "like looking at a pool table with balls randomly scattered around and then trying to reconstruct a game".
- Earth geologists know a solution exists; for a fictional world "you have no such guarantee".
- "rifting and collisions in gplates can sorta be treated as each other in reverse", but you must accept a wide margin of error and be "pretty abstract with your reconstruction of ocean plate motions".
- Another reply calls running it backwards a "pretty different approach which would probably take longer".

https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/06/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-v.html

**Rules of thumb (Part Va)**
- **Active vs. passive margins:**
  - The coast facing the direction of motion is the active margin: deep trench offshore, volcanic orogeny onshore, high range, steep coast.
  - The trailing coast is the passive margin: well-eroded topography, vast coastal plains, wide shelves. It often keeps old eroded mountains.
  https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html
- **Ocean layout after a breakup:** a ring of continents and island arcs. A young interior ocean surrounded by passive margins, with coasts "vaguely mirrored on opposite sides". An older exterior ocean surrounded by active margins. Some continents, like Africa, are "left behind" with passive margins all round. https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html
- **Coastline shape vs. boundary type:**
  - Leading-edge active margins have "fairly smooth coastlines with high coastal ranges".
  - Trailing-edge active margins (slab rollback) have "more jagged coastlines with offshore island arcs".
  - Passive margins have either jagged coastlines "with numerous long inlets from river estuaries reaching into a low coastal plain", or smoother coastlines due to mantle uplift.
  https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html
- **Rift placement:** new rifts often form close to old sutures "but generally not along the exact same lines". So old supercontinent-assembly mountains end up on the new passive margin and new mountains on the active margin. https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html
- **Continent profile (anti-pattern):** "contrary to the common tendency of many inexperienced mappers and simplistic map generators to make continents with high interiors sloping down to low coasts, the more common (but not ubiquitous) tendency is for continents to have low interior basins surrounded by coastal mountain ranges of various ages". This also explains big rivers like the Amazon and Mississippi draining through gaps. https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html
- **Why noise generators produce that anti-pattern** (comment by a reader, not the author): stacked Perlin noise lets the largest layers dominate, so there are no true hierarchical structures. The author adds that fractal generators "are not really good at forming concentrated terrain features". https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html
- **Plate speeds:**
  - A table of four categories: Subducting Ocean, Recent Subduction Collision, Active Margin Continent, Passive Margin Continent. Values as rendered, in order: 10-20, 5-10, 2-5, and <1 cm/yr. The pairing is inferred from the table's row order in the page text.
  - 1 cm/yr is about 0.9 deg per 10 Myr, so an active-margin continent covers about 20-50 deg of longitude per 100 Myr.
  - Author reply: about 1-5 cm/yr for major continents and 2-10 cm/yr for ocean-only plates.
  https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html
- **Summary bullets:**
  - Ocean crust is replaced on ~250 Myr timescales.
  - Continental crust never subducts.
  - The supercontinent cycle is ~750 Myr (perhaps 400-500 Myr recently).
  - Subduction-zone position mainly controls plate motion.
  - Himalaya-like plateaus come from repeated small-fragment impacts followed by a fast big collision.
  - Mediterranean-like seas come from trapped ocean crust.
  https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html
- **Old features fade:** rifts and orogenies are "almost totally erased over 500 million-1 billion year timescales" in terms of their effect on current terrain (author reply). https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html

**Geometry constraints (GPlates supplement replies)**
- A boundary between only 2 plates that spans more than half the planet cannot diverge along its whole length.
- A boundary tracing more than half a circle cannot diverge, and it cannot run into concave bays.
- "the objects here are sections of continental crust, not the actual shorelines".
- Early rift Euler poles lie far from the rift, giving mostly translation with a hinge-like motion.
- Triple junctions start near 60 deg but vary.

https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/06/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-v.html

**Landform catalogue (Part VIIa) linking landform to mechanism**
- Shoreline subduction gives an Andean-type range: a high, thin volcanic front, often an outer-arc ridge and a forearc basin.
- Flat-slab subduction gives a Laramide-type range: a broader plateau.
- Continent collision gives a Ural-type range: high and thin, little volcanism, no outer-arc ridge, possibly a foreland basin.
- Complex collisions of many small landmasses give a Himalayan-type range with a broad high plateau.
- Fold-and-thrust belts form parallel ridges. Basin and Range forms short, ragged ridges.
- Trailing-edge subduction gives an offshore volcanic island line.
- Hotspots give single volcanoes or chains; on land they make broader plateaus.
- Young continental rifts form a long, shallow valley with low flanking ridges and scattered volcanoes.
- LIPs form volcanic plateaus.
- Uplift away from ranges can come from flat-slab subduction, mantle upwelling, or glacial rebound.

https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2021/07/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-viia.html

Dimensions from Part VIIa:
- Arc volcanoes are 100-300 km from the trench and spaced 20-100 km apart.
- Hawaii-type hotspot islands are 50-150 km across and about 100 km apart.
- The East African Rift is over 3,000 km long.
- Fold-belt ridges are 5-50 km apart, with belts of 10-20 ridges.

https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2021/07/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-viia.html

- **Old ranges:** older ranges match the ideal types less and less and can become amorphous highland plateaus (Mexico, Anatolia, SE China). Their edges are higher than their interiors, ranges tend to run parallel to the boundary that formed them, and there is a fall line. https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2021/07/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-viia.html
- **Earth's two active belts:** the Pacific ring and the Indonesia-Himalaya-Alps-Atlas spur. https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2021/07/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-viia.html

**Terrain pipeline (Part VIIc)**
- Inputs from GPlates: land/sea mask; orogenies coloured by age plus cratons and failed rifts; LIPs, hotspots and hotspot paths; subduction zones and terrane outlines. Plus a rough topography and an ExoPlaSim climate map.
- Equirectangular at 3,600x1,800 px.
- Reproject per continent with G.Projector (oblique Hammer).
- Erosion in Wilbur or gospl.
- The post is "more art than science". It lists as missing: glacial action, endorheic basins and lakes, basin detection, and coastal erosion.

https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2023/03/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-viic.html

**Tool complaints (GPlates)**
- "primarily designed for use by geologists"; worldbuilders "find it awkward"; many tools are for Earth evidence and are not useful for new worlds.
- GPlates reads the rotation file backwards from the present, which makes forward simulation counterintuitive.
- Flowlines drift (a bug).
- Changing a feature's plate ID makes it jump.
- Topologies have to be fixed frame by frame ("It's as tedious as it sounds").
- One run takes the author 1 week full-time, typically 2-4 weeks.
- Don't edit positions at 0 Ma.

https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/06/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-v.html ; https://worldbuildingpasta.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-apple-pie-from-scratch-part-va.html

- WBP's welcome post credits a reddit "curved lines" method as "particularly clever ... if you're in a hurry": https://www.reddit.com/r/worldbuilding/comments/7et6b6/semirealistic_geography_for_dummies_or_how_i/ . ### 2.2 Artifexian (YouTube)

**Canonical community wiki Q 247575, "Guidance: How to Create Sensible Plate Tectonics"**
- The question notes that WB.SE "regularly hosts questions asking either how to establish plate tectonics based on an existing planetary map". https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/questions/247575
- Rules collected in the answer (https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/247576):
  - Start from a chart of Earth's plates.
  - Find "coasts that look like they could fit into a different coast on an adjacent continent". This is a jigsaw-fit test.
  - Plates never meet at a 4-way "t".
  - Boundaries are "many joined roughly 120 degree 3 way junctions, right angle T, or large smooth arcs of subduction island arcs (Aleutian islands, Java)". An island arc should sit on a subduction zone.
  - Plates "rarely cross in the middle of landmasses". If one does, it implies a long collision with a range bisecting the continent.
  - A typical plate is ocean encompassing a landmass. The South American plate runs from the Andes to the mid-Atlantic ridge.
  - Fault lines run parallel to ridge lines and to lines of deep-water points.
  - Islands pop up along fault lines.
  - High and low spots also occur away from boundaries (impacts, erosion, karst).
  - "If your map is only showing coast lines, it doesn't show us topography, which is really what we need to identify plates. You can identify tectonic plates and derive mountains from them, or set your mountain ranges to identify plates."
  - Plates contain oceanic crust as well as continental crust.
  - Divergent boundaries have the same crust type on both sides.
  - Note the internal contradiction: "never a T" versus "right angle T". Contributors are mostly non-geologists.
- Similar answers: https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/246666 ; https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/246679 ; https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/223438

**Q 116289, "Starting with a map of current landforms, how can I write the geographic history of my planet?"**
- Answer 116299 gives steps:
  1. Classify each current boundary as subduction, head-on collision or lateral.
  2. Define the rifts (where plates form).
  3. Some plates just "float around".
  4. Set speeds and run backwards: when plates separate in reverse, "a mountain is lost and maybe a sea appears".
- Its conclusion: "is easier to go forward instead of backwards. Because rifts follow certain rules that are hard to fulfill if you go backwards, specially when you have to deal with the rule of triple junction."
- https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/116299
- Answer 116290: run geomorphic clues backwards. V-shaped valleys are fluvial, U-shaped glacial, moraines mark ice limits, sediments mark sea or river mouths. Gives the proto-Amazon reversal by Andean uplift as an example. https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/116290

**Fractal-generator maps (Q 223432, Q 254619)**
- A "fractal generated map" is geologically homogeneous, with no plates. A moth-eaten coast with many islands suggests flat, partly inundated lowland, not a tectonic landscape. https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/223475
- Plates drawn over such a map do not produce the existing landforms. The answer recommends building from first principles. https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/255658

**Q 220646**
- Common mistake: almost every continent made of two plates colliding, with a range down the middle. Continent-continent convergence is rare.
- Equally sized colliding continents would need perfectly matching edges, which "is just not going to happen".
- https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/220659
- Another answer: coastlines in mid-plate give a wide shelf (east South America); coastlines at plate margins give a Chilean-style coast.
- https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/220747

**Q 198365 (isolated high range far from boundaries)**
- Possible explanations: a relict collision (Ural analogue), the river divide placed at the range, a hotspot chain, or antipodal impact terrain. https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/198369 ; https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/198367 ; https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/198366

**Q 106664 (continents drawn to fit a supercontinent)**
- Matching mountain belts across an ocean (Appalachians/Caledonides/Atlas).
- Rifted margins can have steep escarpments facing the ocean (African Great Escarpment, the Ghats, the Australian Alps).
- Forward-moving continents gain volcanic islands in front.
- https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/106745 ; https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/106665

**Q 118177**
- Island chains are expected at triple-plate borders, inlets at boundaries, and ranges continuing along boundaries. https://worldbuilding.stackexchange.com/a/118185

**Q 77976**
- Coastlines should vary in fractal dimension along their length (GB 1.25, Ireland 1.22, Norway 1.52), tied to rock type.

### 2.6 Other tutorials and guides

- Exilian (Jubal):
  - Big mountains, earthquake zones and most volcanoes must lie on a current or former plate boundary.
  - Think in natural lines, not circles.
  - Peninsulas are often bounded by mountains (India/Himalaya, Italy/Alps, Iberia/Pyrenees).
  - Shoreline ranges come from oceanic subduction.
  - Rivers form basins with watersheds and end in estuaries or deltas, not at right angles to the coast.
  - Lakes sit at basin bottoms. Bays sit in soft rock and headlands in hard rock.
  https://exilian.co.uk/article.php?id=116180
- Go Teen Writers (2022):
  - Fold mountains are staggered and curving.
  - Rain shadows: the wet side faces the ocean.
  - "mountains do not exist by themselves unless they are near hot spots", so a Lonely Mountain should be volcanic.
  - Residual mountains are remnants of old ranges.
  - Draw imaginary plates along the coast to inspire coastal features.
  https://goteenwriters.com/2022/10/19/geography-for-worldbuilding-part-1/
- Amy Laurens (2016): "The mountains are where things crash together"; notes the Great Dividing Range in mid-plate Australia as an exception. https://amylaurens.com/2016/08/how-to-build-a-world-from-the-ground-up-week-3-tall-pointy-things/
- rpgbot:
  - Scrap-paper method: tear plates, overlap them; overlaps make mountains or island chains, gaps make volcanoes.
  - Mentions Tectonics.js ("doesn't show elevation very well").
  - Rules: rivers do not split except in deltas; lakes have one outlet.
  - Some boundary claims are simplistic (divergent = volcano chains).
  https://rpgbot.net/general/worldbuilding/planetarygeography
- Inkwell Ideas, 2026 "Top 10 Mistakes": "Mountains are usually in ranges, and based on plate tectonics"; exceptions exist ("rivers can split"); save "Magic!" for genuine misses. https://inkwellideas.com/2026/05/top-10-mistakes-of-fantasy-map-making/
- Acks on Panem (active vs. passive margins):
  - Active margins have mountains and elevation, so they resist sea-level rise.
  - Passive margins are eroded to a gentle slope near base level, so sea-level rise floods far inland.
  - West American ranges get younger eastward (flat-slab hypothesis).
  https://reactormag.com/what-the-world-of-the-hunger-games-teaches-us-about-global-warming/

### 2.7 Consolidated lists

**(1) Recipes for working backwards from a coastline**
- **WBP:**
  1. Jigsaw-test the continents back into a supercontinent, allowing one or two outliers.
  2. Plates move away from the old supercontinent centre.
  3. Put active margins and current orogenies on the leading edges.
  4. Put passive margins on the coasts facing the young interior ocean.
  5. Place older mountains artistically.
  6. Check the feasibility test: no orphan landmasses far from subduction, and leading edges must be active.
  (2.1)
- **GoT (Zahirovic):**
  1. Digitise the fixed coastline (GIS).
  2. Mark subduction and convergence wherever there is volcanism and mountains.
  3. Close the youngest ocean first (Narrow Sea at 25 Ma, East African Rift analogue).
  4. Go deeper with growing uncertainty.
  5. Animate in GPlates.
  (1.1)
- **WB.SE 116299:**
  1. Classify the current boundaries.
  2. Locate the rifts.
  3. Assign speeds.
  4. Reverse: separations remove mountains and create seas. Watch triple junctions.
  (2.3)
- **Sarjeant / Mapsburgh style:**
  1. Each range becomes a suture or margin.
  2. Range junctions become triple junctions.
  3. Long straight valleys become rifts or aulacogens.
  4. Flat interiors become cratons.
  5. Isolated volcanoes become hotspots, with a chain direction giving plate motion.
  6. Short, broken coastal ranges with no arc are old collisions now on a passive margin.
  (1.3, 1.4)

**(2) Rules of thumb: coastline and landform to boundary type or history**
- A smooth coast with a high coastal range is a leading-edge active margin. A jagged coast with an offshore island arc is a trailing-edge (rollback) margin. A jagged coast with estuary inlets and a broad plain, or a smooth uplifted coast, is a passive margin. (WBP Va)
- Mirrored or jigsaw coasts across an ocean mean a young rifted ocean. (WBP Va; WB.SE 247576; GoT)
- A peninsula bounded by mountains at its root is an indenter (India, Italy, Iberia). (Exilian)
- A mountain belt inside a continent is a suture (old collision). Height and ruggedness indicate age. (Acks; EGU 2025; WBP VIIa)
- An arc of islands is a subduction zone. Hotspot chains are age-progressive and point along plate motion. (WB.SE 247576; Acks; Mapsburgh)
- A long straight valley or sea inlet is a rift or failed rift. (Reynolds/Sarjeant; WB.SE 78011)
- Cliffed straight coasts can be rifted margins (Bunda Cliffs); escarpments appear on rifted margins. (WB.SE 191911, 106745)
- A broad high plateau needs a complex multi-collision or flat-slab subduction, or a LIP/hotspot. (WBP Va/VIIa)
- An inland sea or lake ringed by mountains is an endorheic basin, as in Mordor/Nurnen and Tarim. (Exilian; Rafferty)
- An enclosed sea between converging continents is trapped ocean (Mediterranean). (WBP Va)
- A mid-plate continent is geologically inert (Australia). (WB.SE 147082)

**(3) Common mistakes warned about**
- High-interior "spine" continents sloping to low coasts. Reality: low interior basins ringed by coastal ranges of various ages. (WBP Va)
- Fractal or noise maps with no concentrated terrain features or hierarchy. (WBP comments; WB.SE 223475, 255658)
- Every continent split by a central collision range. (WB.SE 220659)
- Right-angle range corners and boxed basins (Mordor). (Acks; Gibson)
- Isolated inland volcanoes with no hotspot track. (Acks)
- A coastal range that ends abruptly with no continuation along the coast and no offshore arc, which argues against active subduction. (Mapsburgh)
- 4-way plate junctions and arrows that contradict each other. (WB.SE 246666; CG snippet)
- Plate boundaries running along map edges or poles. (WB.SE 223438)
- A boundary spanning more than half the globe diverging along its whole length; divergent boundaries into concave bays. (WBP supplement)
- Rivers parallel to ranges for long distances; one-sided tributaries; inland deltas; rivers crossing ranges without antecedence; lakes with no watershed; rivers splitting; rivers joining two oceans; rivers entering the sea at right angles. (Acks; rpgbot; Exilian)
- Plateaus without a mechanism. (implied by WBP VIIa)
- Missing rain shadows. (Go Teen Writers; D&D Beyond snippet)

**(4) Tools used and complaints**
- GPlates: academic and awkward. It reads rotations backwards from the present (handy for retrofit, confusing forwards). Flowlines drift, IDs jump, topologies are tedious, and a run takes 1-4 weeks. (WBP supplement)
- Tectonics.js: weak elevation display (rpgbot). A WB.SE user could not infer plates from its output (WB.SE 246679).
- Wilbur and gospl for erosion; G.Projector for reprojection; ExoPlaSim for climate. Missing: lakes, endorheic basins, glacial effects, coastal erosion. (WBP VIIc)
- Azgaar: can keep the coastline fixed, but has no tectonic logic. (2.5)
- The core unmet need: no tool takes a fixed coastline and proposes plates and a history. WBP says reverse retrofitting needs "a pretty different approach" and a wide margin of error. CG posters gave up on backward work. (2.1, 2.4)

## Key takeaways for a tool that keeps a fixed coastline and invents a tectonic history

**Inference rules worth encoding**
1. **Close the youngest oceans first.** Pair up coastlines that fit like a jigsaw, with matching shape and mirrored passive margins, and treat them as the youngest rift. Undo that spreading before anything else. This is the "easiest part" according to the GoT team. Use a fit score that compares shapes at shelf or shelf-edge scale.
2. **Look for a supercontinent.** Test whether all landmasses (minus one or two) can be reassembled. Present plate velocities then point away from the assembly centre.
3. **Classify every coast segment by margin type:**
   - Leading edge: active, smooth, high coastal range, trench.
   - Trailing edge with an arc: rollback.
   - Facing a young interior ocean: passive, wide shelf, plains, estuaries.
   Derived from the WBP coast-shape rules plus direction of motion.
4. **Mountains are evidence, not decoration:**
   - A coastal range means subduction (Andean type).
   - An interior linear belt means a suture.
   - A broad plateau means multi-collision or flat slab.
   - Short, broken or low coastal ranges with no arc mean an old orogen now on a passive margin.
   Encode age from height and ruggedness.
5. **Boundary topology constraints:**
   - Only triple junctions, near 120 deg, roughly 60 deg at birth.
   - No 4-way junctions.
   - Smooth arcs for subduction.
   - A 2-plate divergent boundary cannot exceed a hemisphere or enter concave bays.
   - Boundaries rarely cut through continents unless a range marks them.
   - Most plates are mixed continental and oceanic.
   - Use roughly 12-18 plates for an Earth-sized world. The WB.SE answer suggests 12-18 shapes, and Frozenfractal notes Earth has ~15 plates for 6 continents.
6. **Speeds and timescales for plausibility checks:**
   - Ocean plates 2-10 cm/yr; continents 1-5 cm/yr; passive-margin continents under 1 cm/yr.
   - 1 cm/yr is about 0.9 deg per 10 Myr.
   - Ocean crust is recycled every ~250 Myr.
   - Supercontinent cycle ~400-750 Myr.
   - Tectonic features are mostly erased after 0.5-1 Gyr.
7. **Isolated volcanoes and islands** become hotspots that need an age-progressive track oriented along the plate's motion. Island arcs must sit on subduction zones.
8. **Rifts** reuse old sutures but not exactly. Long straight valleys or inlets become rifts or aulacogens. Rifted margins can carry escarpments or cliffs.
9. **Topography output:** prefer low interior basins ringed by ranges, and a drainage network consistent with the ranges (Acks rules). For a quick default heightmap from a drawn map, Lunt's class elevations (mountain 4000 m, foothills 2000 m, plain 300 m, rivers 100 m, ocean deepening to 4000 m) are a documented baseline.

**Pitfalls**
- The solution is not unique. Earth geologists know a solution exists; a fictional map may have none. The tool should present several candidate histories with confidence levels, flag contradictions instead of forcing a fit, and accept a wide margin of error, especially for oceans.
- Uncertainty grows with age. Beyond the last supercontinent (~250 Ma for Earth) the evidence is weak, so state the liberties explicitly, as the GoT and Lunt papers did.
- Coastlines are not crustal boundaries. Shelves and continental crust extend beyond the shoreline. Sea level and coastal erosion change the outline.
- Noise or fractal coastlines carry little tectonic signal. Moth-eaten coasts with many islands suggest a flooded lowland, so do not over-read them.
- Fixed features may be impossible (right-angle ranges, boxed basins, lonely inland volcanoes). The tool should offer rare real analogues (Tarim, Anatolia microplate, antecedent rivers) or an explicit "non-tectonic or magic" flag.
- Community rules contradict each other and include errors (for example "never a T" versus "right-angle T"; transform faults causing volcanoes). Encode the rules backed by geologists (WBP, Acks, EGU), not forum folklore.
- Existing tools force a forward workflow (GPlates, Artifexian, WBP) or have no tectonics (Azgaar). The gap the tool should fill is a backward or retrofit workflow that keeps the coastline locked.
