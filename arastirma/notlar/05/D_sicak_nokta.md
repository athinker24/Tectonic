## 1. Hotspot tracks and ages

- Hawaiian-Emperor chain, hotspot-relative age-distance slope: 57 +/- 2 km/Myr between ~57 and 25 Ma over the central ~1900 km, including the Hawaiian-Emperor Bend (HEB). Hawaiian age progression faster since ~15 Ma (possibly ~27 Ma). Linear age-distance relation may extend to ~82 Ma. READ (abstract): https://epic.awi.de/id/eprint/35519/
  - Derived by us, not stated in the source: 57 km/Myr = 5.7 cm/yr hotspot-relative.
- HEB volcanism started at >=47.5 Ma and lasted >=5 Myr; change in hotspot motion ~55-50 Ma. Same source.
- Midway is ~28.7 Ma (O'Connor et al. 2013 full text, grepped): https://epic.awi.de/35519/1/OConnor-2013-g3-HawaiiSmtAges.pdf
- Bend age: 47-47.5 Ma (O'Connor 2013, READ in part); Sharp and Clague 2006 gave ~50 Ma (SNIPPET, title quoted in search summary). Chain bend angle ~120 degrees (SNIPPET, Wikipedia-type source).
- Chain length: ~6000 km, >100 volcanoes, 82 Myr (Garcia et al. 2015, SNIPPET only: https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/GG/FACULTY/garcia/publications/Garcia_et al-2015_NWHR_GSA.pdf , FAILED fetch). Emperor ~2500 km; NW Hawaiian Ridge ~2800 km (same). Wikipedia ~6200 km (secondary).
- Oldest dated Emperor seamount: Detroit 81 Ma (SNIPPET).
- Island ages, Clague and Dalrymple 1987 (USGS PP 1350), via EarthRef reproduction (SECONDARY, SNIPPET, table page not readable): Kauai 5.1 Ma; Waianae (Oahu) 3.7; Koolau 2.6; West Molokai 1.9; East Molokai 1.76; West Maui 1.32; Lanai 1.28; Kahoolawe 1.03; Haleakala 0.75; Kohala 0.43; Mauna Kea 0.375; Kilauea 0. Source: search result citing earthref.org (ERDA download pages, not readable).
- Yellowstone track: early rate 62 km/Myr (17-10 Ma, ~6.2 cm/yr) and 25 km/Myr (10-2 Ma, ~2.5 cm/yr) from Pierce and Morgan 2009 (SNIPPET; PDF on Durham host failed TLS). Rhyolite migration 2.27 +/- 0.21 cm/yr over last 10 Ma (Anders et al. 2019, SNIPPET). Plume head ~17 Ma (SNIPPET).
- Reunion / Deccan: Richards, Duncan and Courtillot 1989 plume-head/plume-tail model (SNIPPET). Deccan 65-60 Ma and Rajmahal ~116 Ma (Kerguelen link, Mahoney et al. 1983, SNIPPET). Sheth 1999 disputes a Reunion plume role in Deccan volcanism (SNIPPET). Ar-Ar total duration >=8 Myr argued by Sheth (SNIPPET). The Reunion track ages: not read.
- Canary Islands (SNIPPET, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmid/23838703 not read): age progression crude; Lanzarote-Fuerteventura 15-23 Ma to El Hierro <1.1 Ma; plate velocity 12 mm/yr quoted (not checked); Canary seamount province ~1300 km long, ~350 km wide, ~140 Ma. Madeira-Canary average age progression ~1.2 cm/yr (SNIPPET). Single fixed plume model contested.
- Iceland (SNIPPET): Ito, Lin and Gable 1996 (EPSL): Reykjanes swell may be off-axis subsidence rather than a broad dome. Howell et al. 2014 EPSL plume flux 100-200 m3/s (SNIPPET). Crust up to ~40 km thick (Lamont-Doherty 1999 snippet). Plume excess temperature contested (SNIPPET, Foulger critique). No Iceland 2 km dynamic topography figure found.
- Society, Marquesas, Cape Verde: NOT FOUND in any search result. GAP.
- Pacific plate velocity over Hawaii: no present-day value found in a source. Classroom estimates 8.14 cm/yr (textbook snippet) and 13.5 cm/yr (Maricopa text: 54 Myr to 400 kyr division, which is a flawed method per our reading). Caltech snippet: Pacific hotspot frame not fixed; 5-80 mm/yr motions in Cenozoic. AGU 2014 abstract: >40 mm/yr Hawaiian hotspot motion from Emperor paleomagnetism (SNIPPET). Brief's 5-10 cm/yr not confirmed; O'Connor's 5.7 cm/yr (derived) lies inside it.

## 2. Volcanic island heights and subsidence

- Mauna Kea summit: 4,207 m above sea level, shield, most recent eruption 6,000-4,000 yr ago, flanks twice as steep as Mauna Loa. READ: https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/mauna-kea
- Mauna Kea total height from the seafloor: ~10,200 m total, ~4,205 m above sea level (Guinness World Records, SNIPPET: https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/tallest-mountain ). Base definition contested. Submarine part quoted as ~6 km in one snippet (UNVERIFIED). The brief's "10 km from ocean floor" is consistent with this secondary figure but not confirmed from a primary source.
- Subsidence: Moore (1987) (SECONDARY, via a 2018 Frontiers snippet): Hawaiian volcanoes subside 2-4 km by the time they reach sea level; rate decreases after leaving the hotspot. Island of Hawaii west coast drowned-reef/terrace rates 1.9-2.4 mm/yr, steady since ~600 ka (SNIPPET).
- Flexure moat: ten Brink and Watts 1985 Nature, seismic profile of flexural moat flanking Oahu (SNIPPET). Kauai rejuvenated volcanism records flexure over ~2 Myr (SNIPPET).
- Seamount heights: altimetry detects seamounts taller than ~1.5 km; intermediate heights 1-1.5 km are the detection gap (Wessel, Sandwell, Kim 2010). READ: https://tos.org/oceanography/assets/docs/23-1_wessel.pdf
  - Catalogues: ~13,000 seamounts (altimetry); Wessel 2001 ~15,000, revised to ~12,000 after duplicates.
  - Predicted: >100,000 seamounts >1 km height uncharted; speculatively 25 million >100 m.
  - Kitchingman et al. 2007: up to 142,000 seamounts >100 m (cited in Wessel 2010).
  - Height-frequency: exponential falloff with size (Wessel 2010 text, partial).
  - CORRECTION: an earlier memory figure of ~14,500 seamounts above 1000 m is not supported by any source read. Discard it.
  - Kim and Wessel 2011 (SNIPPET): 40,000-55,000 seamounts incl. small ones. UNVERIFIED.

## 3. Hotspot swell (oceanic)

- Swell dimensions: "roughly 3000 km long and 1000 km wide" (Hawaiian Swell, citing Li et al. 2004). READ (secondary thesis): https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/520b1794-221c-42df-a1b9-5e0f5162edf3/content
- Swell height range: Crough (1983) compiled swell heights 500-1200 m and widths 1000-1500 km (SNIPPET via Sleep-related summary). Wessel (1993) sets a swell profile shape with height H (absolute Wessel value not recovered). Wessel 1993 JGR 98(B9):16095, doi 10.1029/93JB01230 (abstract SNIPPET via EarthRef: https://earthref.org/ERR/39754/ ): high geoid-to-topography ratio over the SE chain, falling away from the current hotspot; could reflect time variation in hotspot strength. Elastic thickness 33 km (secondary).
- Buoyancy flux:
  - Davies 1988 (Crough heights, 1000 km width): 6300 kg/s (READ in thesis).
  - Sleep 1990: 8700 kg/s (READ in thesis; Sleep 1990 JGR not read directly).
  - King and Adams 2014: 4660-7100 kg/s recent (READ in thesis).
  - Thesis present-day estimate ~6000-8000 kg/s.
  - Scaling: flux proportional to swell width x height x plate velocity (Durham summary, SNIPPET). Thesis shows flux from swell volume and from swell geometry (height, width) with non-Newtonian rheology.
  - Ribe and Christensen 1994 EPSL 121:435: thermal buoyancy can hold up the swell without lithospheric erosion (SNIPPET). Ribe and Christensen 1999: depletion buoyancy adds ~350 m of uplift on swell axis in reference model (SECONDARY via thesis).
- Mechanisms list (Laske et al. 2011 GJI 187:1725, READ intro): thermal rejuvenation (Detrick and Crough 1978), dynamic support (Watts 1976), thermal erosion (Li et al. 2004), compositional buoyancy (Jordan 1979), hybrids (Laske et al. 2007). URL: https://igppweb.ucsd.edu/~gabi/papers/gji.dec11-all.pdf . Note: the Laske paper also says the swell was "over 1000 km wide" for the PLUME array context (its own array, not a swell dimension).
- Sleep 1990 (JGR 95): 37 hotspots reviewed; 7 good, 14 fair, 16 poor reliability (SNIPPET). Page range in Durham listing appears wrong (6715-6936); verify.
- Flexural moat and arch: ten Brink and Watts 1985 (SNIPPET). Arch height and width: not found in a primary source (GAP).

## 4. Continental hotspot uplift

- Afar / Ethiopia (Hassan et al. 2020 Geoscience Frontiers 11:1669, READ: https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10228133 ): model Afar plume, initial plume head radius 1000 km. Model domal uplift at eruption: radius ~700 km, amplitude >1200 m, stated as comparable to inferred uplift (He et al. 2003; Pik et al. 2003, 2008; Burke and Gunnell 2008). Model post-eruption topographic swell 800-1000 m (MODEL, not observation).
- Ethiopian plateau: mean elevation ~2500 m asl; present topographic dome since Oligocene; minimum surface uplift ~1200 m related to Afar (Tectonics 2015, SNIPPET; not read).
- Timing disagreement: Pik et al. 2003 early Oligocene uplift 30-25 Ma (SNIPPET, EPSL 215:73, not read); Gani et al. 2007 three uplift phases since ~29 Ma (SNIPPET); later thesis argues rise mostly in last 10 Myr (SNIPPET, low weight). Courtillot et al. 1997: Ethiopian traps ~30 Ma, erupted within <=1 Myr (SNIPPET).
- Ebinger and Sleep 1998 Nature 395:788: single plume beneath Ethiopia explains magmatism and uplift across East Africa (SNIPPET). Later work favours two or more plume heads (George et al. 1998; Rogers et al. 2000) (SNIPPET).
- Yellowstone swell / uplift: the Yellowstone Crescent of High Terrain uplift rates 0.1-0.4 mm/yr (SNIPPET of Pierce 2009 review). The "1 km" Yellowstone swell uplift from the brief was NOT found in any source. UNVERIFIED / not supported.

## 8. Plume dynamic topography vs observed amplitudes

- Hoggard et al. 2016 Nature Geoscience 9:456 (year 2016, not 2017 as in brief). READ abstract: https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/global-dynamic-topography-observations-reveal-limited-influence-o/
  - Predictive models: peak +/-2 km at ~10^4 km wavelength.
  - Observed (2,120 oceanic seismic spot measurements): typical peak +/-1 km at ~10^3 km wavelength; long-wavelength (~10^4 km) observed peaks ~+/-500 m (preprint snippet, not confirmed in abstract text read).
  - Explanation: short-wavelength topography from temperature-driven density in a sub-plate asthenospheric channel; signals change quickly with time (stratigraphy).
- Hassan et al. 2020 model (READ): plume dome ~1.2 km (model), swell 0.8-1 km post-eruption (model).
- Our reading (interpretation, not in a source): the ~1000 km wavelength swell (0.5-1.2 km) matches the Hoggard observed short-wavelength band (+/-1 km, ~10^3 km). Plume domes in models are at the high end of the observed band.

## Key takeaways for the tool

1. Hotspot swell: model as ~1000 km wide (Hawaii ~3000 km long, per thesis citing Li et al. 2004), peak height 0.5-1.2 km (Crough 1983 range, secondary), with Wessel (1993) profile shape. Heights above sea floor for islands are not the same as swell height. Keep the swell and the volcano separate in the generator.
2. Plate-track geometry: hotspot-relative slope 57 km/Myr (5.7 cm/yr) for Hawaii-Emperor 57-25 Ma (O'Connor 2013, primary). Use 5-6 cm/yr as a central value for Pacific-type tracks; 2-6 cm/yr for Yellowstone-type (Pierce 2009 snippet, Anders 2019). Do not use the 13.5 cm/yr Maricopa estimate; method is flawed.
3. Island age vs distance: Kauai 5.1 Ma, Oahu 2.6-3.7, Molokai 1.8-1.9, Maui 1.3, Lanai 1.3, Kohala 0.43, Mauna Kea 0.375, Kilauea 0 (Clague and Dalrymple 1987 via secondary snippet, verify before use).
4. Subsidence: volcanoes lose 2-4 km before reaching sea level (Moore 1987, secondary). Old islands sit lower and are eroded; flexure moat and arch exist (ten Brink and Watts 1985, snippet). Model subsidence with age, exponential decay after leaving hotspot.
5. Volcano heights: Mauna Kea summit 4.2 km above sea level (USGS, primary). Total from seafloor ~10 km (secondary only). Use 4.2 km above sea for islands and treat a ~10 km total as UNVERIFIED, flagged in the code.
6. Seamounts: altimetry sees >1.5 km; a census of ~13,000 catalogued plus >100,000 predicted above 1 km uncharted (Wessel 2010, primary). For random seamounts, use a steep exponential size distribution; the number of seamounts is not a fixed count.
7. LIPs on land: size ranges are wide. Use area 0.15-1.5 Mkm2 and volume 0.2-6.6 Mkm3 (Ross 2005 table), with Siberia as an outlier (1-4 Mkm3 original). Thickness: 1-3.5 km is consistent with Deccan (3.0-3.5 km near centre, ~0.9 km elsewhere). Plateau elevation at eruption: UNVERIFIED, no primary source read for this.
8. Duration: LIP main pulses 0.1-0.8 Myr (CRB ~0.76 Myr, Siberia ~0.3 Myr for two-thirds, Deccan four pulses ~100 kyr each in ~0.7-0.8 Myr total). Total durations can exceed 1 Myr. Use 0.3-1 Myr for the main phase.
9. Oceanic plateaus: OJP 1.9 Mkm2 area; Kerguelen + Broken Ridge 2.3 Mkm2; standing 2-4 km above surrounding floor (Neal 2019, primary). Volume: OJP is disputed (8.4 vs 44-57 Mkm3).
10. Dynamic topography: observed long-wavelength (10^4 km) amplitude ~+/-0.5 km (Hoggard 2016 preprint snippet); observed 10^3 km ~+/-1 km (Hoggard 2016 abstract); plume models ~1.2 km (Hassan 2020, model only). The 0.5-1 km band in the brief is consistent with Hoggard. Plume domes in models are large; keep them as optional at 0.5-1.2 km.
11. Isolated continental volcanoes: sources read support rift or plume settings (Ethiopia-Kenya rift, Afar). Volcano density: GVP counts only; compute per area yourself. For a cratonic isolated volcano with no tectonic cause, no source was found; flag it as a non-physical or hypothetical case in the tool.
12. Disagreements to show in the tool: Deccan thickness (2 km in brief vs 3-3.5 km sources); Ethiopian uplift timing (Oligocene vs late Miocene); OJP volume; seamount count; Hawaii plate speed (5.7 cm/yr derived vs 8 and 13.5 classroom).
