## 1. East African Rift (EAR) and Main Ethiopian Rift (MER)

- Corti et al. 2018, Tectonics, "along-axis variations in architecture, segmentation and evolution" of the MER (read full PDF):
  - ~70% of the 500 km long MER is asymmetric; <half is a classical symmetric rift. Source: https://flore.unifi.it/retrieve/e398c37d-7e04-179a-e053-3705fe0a4cff/Corti_et_al-2018-Tectonics.pdf
  - Border faults are "long (>=50 km), large-offset (typically >500 m)"; they form escarpments separating rift floor from plateaus; activated diachronously over the last 10-12 Ma. Same source.
  - Rift margin elevation drop (m) and mean gradient per sector (Table 1, same source). Examples: Sector 2 (100 km, asymmetric, east master fault) West 1500 m / East 1416 m, gradients 0.019 / 0.042; Sector 5 (100 km, asymmetric east) West 665 m / East 948 m, gradients 0.019 / 0.067; Sector 1 (70 km, symmetric) 1799 / 1750 m. Same source.
  - Guraghe escarpment on NW side of MER ~1500 m high; SE side step-like scarp. Same source.
  - Typical local relief ~1000 m quoted for Ethiopian plateau area (same source, text fragment). Asymmetry gradient difference: rift margin gradients differ by <40% in symmetric sectors, >100% in asymmetric ones. Same source.
  - Effective elastic thickness Te: Ethiopian Plateau Te much lower than Somalian Plateau (where Te >60 km); Te down to 10-15 km at Bonga Goba lineament (citing Perez-Gussinye et al. 2009). Same source.
  - Flexural uplift of footwall plus fluvial unloading form a positive feedback (Corti 2018 discusses; cites Sembroni et al. 2016). Same source.
  - Authors argue asymmetry arises from pre-rift lithospheric asymmetry, "does not require low-angle detachment faults". Same source.
  - Ethiopian plateau: up to ~2 km rock uplift since ~30 Ma, Afar (UNVERIFIED: this figure came from a search snippet of Corti 2015 book chapter, https://flore.unifi.it/bitstream/2158/1136615/1/Corti_2015_springer_book.pdf, not yet read).

- KRISP crustal thickness (from search snippet only, not yet read): crust 20 km beneath Lake Turkana to ~35 km under the Kenya dome culmination near Lake Naivasha. Source (snippet, UNVERIFIED in full): https://typeset.io/pdf/the-east-african-rift-system-in-the-light-of-krisp-90-on2mgynzpc.pdf

- Koptev et al. 2018 (Geophys. J. Int.?): search snippet gives wide rift 200-350 km in Turkana depression between Kenyan and Ethiopian domes; narrow Kenya rift at Tanzanian craton margin. PDF NOT fetched: TLS certificate error on https://www.geologie.ens.fr/~ecalais/publications/koptev_et_al-2018-geophysic.pdf (not bypassed). Snippet only, UNVERIFIED.

## 2. Aegean and Corinth

- Armijo et al. 2003, EPSL 217, 85-95 "Linear elastic fracture mechanics explains the past and present evolution of the Aegean" (read full PDF): extension in Aegean began 20-15 Ma; North Anatolian Fault born 10-13 Ma in eastern Turkey; Corinth Rift increased from ~1 mm/yr to ~10 mm/yr at ~1 Ma (citing Armijo et al. 1996). The authors attribute the increase to NAF propagation and more distributed deformation ahead of it, NOT to slab pull. Source: https://www.ipgp.fr/~armijo/ArmijoPDF/ArmijoEPLS03.pdf
  - CORRECTION: an earlier WebSearch snippet said the 1 to 10 mm/yr jump was linked to slab pull. The paper text I read attributes it to NAF propagation. Trust the paper text.

- Tiberi et al. 2001 GJI (HAL PDF URL returned an HTML landing page, not a PDF; not read): search snippet says ~1 cm/yr average extension over last 100 years geodetically (N10E), max crustal thinning north of Gulf of Corinth, low-angle normal fault at 10-15 km depth. UNVERIFIED. Source: https://hal-insu.archives-ouvertes.fr/insu-01356363/file/Geophys.%20J.%20Int.-2001-Tiberi-797-808.pdf (landing page only)

- Reilinger et al. 2010 (search snippet only): North Anatolian fault propagated across northern Aegean in the last 1-4 Ma, opening Gulfs of Evia and Corinth; low present GPS strain rates vs geologic crustal thinning in the south Aegean. Not read.

## 3. Basin and Range (BR) and core complexes

- GSA Today 2023, vol. 33(3-4), "Metamorphic Core Complex Dichotomy in the North American Cordillera Explained by Buoyant Upwelling in Variably Thick Crust" (read full PDF; authors not extracted): Metamorphic core complexes in the North American Cordillera show two spacings. Hinterland (north, Paleogene, thickened orogenic plateau) MCCs have characteristic spacing ~200 km; foreland (south, thinner crust, Oligocene-Miocene) MCCs closely spaced ~50 km, ~4:1 ratio. Source: https://Geosociety.org/gsatoday/science/G548A/GSATG548A.pdf

## 4. Baikal rift (read Logachev 1993 review, full text)

- Logachev 1993 "History and geodynamics of the Lake Baikal rift in the context of the Eastern Siberia rift system: a review" (Elf01 PDF): Lake Baikal 636 km long, average width 40-50 km (max 70 km), maximum depth 1637 m, bottom 1181 m below sea level. Source: https://www.africamuseum.be/publication_docs/Elf01-Logachev.pdf
  - DISAGREEMENT: 1642 m (commonly quoted, and the search snippet from Wikipedia) vs 1637 m (Logachev 1993). Search snippet also gives max width 79 km; Logachev gives max 70 km.
- Baikal basin (central segment) ~680 km long, ~1/3 of total rift zone. Other eleven rift basins: 70-190 km long and 25-40 km wide. Same source.
- Sediment thickness: max 7500-8000 m in Baikal basin, decreasing to 500-1000 m at rift system limits. Same source. (Search snippet says >9 km around Selenga delta; Logachev text says 3100 m penetrated in Selenga delta boreholes.)
- Upper unit (Pleistocene-Holocene) sediments 1000-1200 m thick; sedimentation rate during the "fast rifting" stage 5-10 times the earlier Eocene-Oligocene-Miocene stage. Same source.
- Rift shoulder uplift: "considerable (1500-2000 m) uplift of rift shoulders" during fast rifting stage. Same source. Shoulder ridges of 2500-3000 m altitude mentioned. Same source.
- Seismicity: Logachev says Baikal rift seismicity is higher than all other continental rifts (citing Lipman et al. 1989). Same source.
- Baikal rate: search snippets disagree (2 cm/yr widening vs ~4 mm/yr crustal extension). Not verified in a primary source yet. UNVERIFIED.
- Tanganyika 1470 m depth: NOT verified in any source read. Search returned no depth figure. UNVERIFIED.

## 5. Upper Rhine Graben (URG)

- Schumacher 2002 is in Tectonics 21 (not Tectonophysics as the brief says): "Upper Rhine Graben: Role of preexisting structures during rift evolution". Not read (search snippet only).
- Snippet (secondary): URG ~320 km long; average width ~36 km (citing Mayer et al. 1997); Hinsken et al. 2007 width 35 to >60 km, controls crustal stretching factor. Source: https://www.tecto.earth.unibas.ch/publications/Hinskenetal2007.pdf (read below)
- European Cenozoic Rift System Moho depth 24-36 km (snippet, unverified).

- Hinsken, Ustaszewski, Wetzel 2007, Int J Earth Sci 96:979-1002 "Graben width controlling syn-rift sedimentation: the Palaeogene southern Upper Rhine Graben" (read full PDF): horizontal extension DL ~4-5 km estimated for URG from numerous cross-sections (roughly constant); rift width Lf varies 35 to >60 km, giving variable crustal stretching factor; along-strike width variation controls depocentre (Potash Basin, deep trough). Source: https://www.tecto.earth.unibas.ch/publications/Hinskenetal2007.pdf
  - Lengthbalanced extension in one section 4.6 km vs 4.2 km (same source).
  - Graben-wide Moho: "decreased crustal thickness of 24 km below the Mulhouse Potash Salt Basin" under a regional topographic dome (same source).
  - Crustal thickness/stretch factor values for URG not given numerically beyond this. Stretching factor beta = L0/Lf type relation is implied (not stated numerically in the excerpt).
  - Note: the snippet's "average width 36 km" (Mayer et al. 1997) is consistent with the 35 km lower bound.

## 7. Afar (read Bastow and Keir 2011, Nature Geoscience 4, 248-250, PDF)

- Crust abruptly thins to <15 km in northern Afar; upper-mantle velocities 7.4-7.5 km/s. Source: https://flore.unifi.it/bitstream/2158/1110138/1/ngeo1095.pdf (DOI 10.1038/NGEO1095)
- Danakil basin filled by up to ~3 km Pliocene-Recent evaporites and basalt; subsidence below sea level no older than ~5 Myr. Same source.
- Effective elastic thickness Te ~5 km in northernmost Afar vs ~9 km farther south. Same source.
- Extension accommodated by magma intrusion without marked plate thinning implies low stretching factors early in breakup. Same source.
- A figure label lists 16.4 mm/yr near Erta Ale (rate meaning not stated in extracted text; UNVERIFIED as extension rate). Same source.

- Ebinger & Casey 2001 Geology 29, 527-530: magmatic segment ~20 km wide, ~60 km long (snippet only, UNVERIFIED).

## 9. Aegean (Jolivet 2013 review read; Jolivet 2010 EPSL PDF was HTML landing, not read)

- Jolivet et al. 2013, Tectonophysics (read full PDF): Moho depth decreases from ~35 km below central Anatolia to ~30 km below the Menderes Massif, to max ~25 km below the Cyclades, and ~15 km below the North Aegean Trough and Cretan Sea (citing Mutlu and Karabulut 2011; Bohnhoff et al. 2001; Makris 1978; Tirel et al. 2004b). Source: https://web.itu.edu.tr/~okay/makalelerim/114_jolivet_et_al_2013_aegean_tectonics_tectonophysics.pdf
  - Finite stretching and crustal thinning stronger in the central Aegean than in western Turkey. Same source.
  - Aegean volcanic arc migrated south "at a constant rate of ~3 cm/yr" after 35 Ma (Jolivet and Brun 2010). This is ARC MIGRATION, not extension rate. Same source.
  - Extension began ~35 Ma (drastic change) in the Aegean region; Rhodope extension earlier. Same source.
  - Core complex sequence: Rhodope (Eocene), Cyclades (Oligocene-Miocene), then localisation in western Turkey, Corinth Rift and external Hellenic arc after Messinian. Same source (and search snippet).
  - Cyclades detachment displacements ~50-100 km (EGU 2024 snippet only, UNVERIFIED).
  - No Cyclades extension rate in mm/yr extracted; the "2 cm/yr" figure from the brief is UNVERIFIED.
- Armijo 2003 (section 2) remains the source for Corinth 1 to 10 mm/yr.
- Tirel et al. 2009 model boundary velocity 2.0-2.3 cm/yr (snippet, modelled, not read).

## 10. Failed rifts and North American rifts

- Elling et al. 2022, GSA Today 32(6) "Three Major Failed Rifts in Central North America: Similarities and Differences" (read full PDF): all three rifts (Midcontinent Rift MCR, Southern Oklahoma Aulacogen SOA, Reelfoot Rift RR) are ~60-80 km wide. Source: https://Geosociety.org/gsatoday/science/G518A/GSATG518A.pdf
  - MCR ~3000 km long, >2 million km3 of buried igneous rock; formed ~1.1 Ga (Rodinia). Same source (p. with "MCR is ~3000 km long").
  - RR: 70 km wide and more than 300 km long NE-trending graben. Same source.
  - SOA and RR initiated ~560 Ma during breakup of Rodinia; SOA experienced up to 15 km of inversion in the late Paleozoic. Same source.
  - Average cross-sectional areas: MCR arms 1100 and 680 km2 (per arm), SOA 470 km2 average. Volcanic fills: MCR east arm 10-15 km, west arm 20-25 km of volcanics; RR gravity anomaly only 10-15 mGal. Same source.
  - Benue trough dimensions: NOT FOUND in any source. UNVERIFIED / open.
- Reelfoot dimensions: 70 km wide, >300 km long (above).

## 12. Basin and Range (Speed 1979, USGS OF 79-1239 ch 1-6, read)

- Speed 1979 "Extensional faulting in the Great Basin: kinematics and possible changes with depth" (read): average spacing of main Great Basin normal faults 15-20 km, forming the linear ranges ("worms marching toward Mexico"). Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1979/of79-1239/Chapters/CH1-06.PDF
  - Blocks in irregular arrays ~4-5 km (same source).
  - Brittle upper layer <5 km thick, closely spaced faults ramp into intermediate layer from ~5 to 15-20 km depth (same source).
  - "Ancient sialic crust, 25-30 km thick" underlies Great Basin east and south of a pre-Tertiary suture (same source). DISAGREEMENT with 30-35 km (wikipedia) and 30-35 km from 1987 snippet.
  - Fault throw: "as great as 3 km, exceptionally 5 km" for some faults (same source, line ~139, context uncertain).
  - Local statement: "spacing of main faults about 1 per 5 km over 25-30 km normal to strike" in one local area (same source).
- Stewart 1971 and 1980 (snippet only): tilt of ranges averages 15-20 deg; extension 20-30% for Great Basin (secondary).
- Range spacing 25-35 km and 1-2 km ridge relief in brief: NOT confirmed. Speed 1979 gives 15-20 km fault spacing, which is a different measure from range spacing. UNVERIFIED.

## 18. Key takeaways for the tool

1. Rift width (not a single number): Rhine Graben 35 to >60 km (Hinsken 2007); Elling et al. 2022: all three North American failed rifts ~60-80 km wide, RR 70 km by >300 km; Baikal basins 25-40 km wide, lake 40-50 km (max 70) (Logachev 1993); Ethiopia MER asymmetric 500 km; Western Rift extension up to ~30 km in 7 Ma (Delvaux 2016); Ebinger snippets say 40-75 km for Kenya/Western zones (UNVERIFIED primary); flank uplift zones 150-400 km (snippet, disagrees).
2. Brittle layer: Speed 1979 gives brittle upper layer <5 km thick in Great Basin, closely spaced faults 5 to 15-20 km depth. Use ~5 km brittle layer for Basin and Range-type crust. Scaling of width versus brittle thickness is NOT confirmed by a source read. Lavier et al. 2000 and Lin & Parmentier 1990 are the leads but unreachable (cert error).
3. Fault and range spacing: Great Basin main fault spacing 15-20 km (Speed 1979); 25-35 km range spacing in the brief NOT confirmed. Basement master-fault spacing 15-20 km for Permo-Triassic North Sea rifts (Faerseth 1995). MCC spacing: 50 km (foreland, thin crust) to 200 km (hinterland, thick crust) (GSA Today 2023 MCC paper). Use these as the scale range.
4. Crust thickness: BR 25-30 km (Speed 1979) vs 30-35 km (snippets) vs ~20% thinning from 37 to 31 km (snippet). Rio Grande axis 28-30 km rising to 35 km SW NM (Averill 2007 via GSA Today 2021). Aegean Moho ~35 km central Anatolia, 30 km Menderes, ~25 km Cyclades, ~15 km N Aegean Trough and Cretan Sea (Jolivet 2013). Northern Afar <15 km (Bastow & Keir 2011). Turkana ~20 km, Kenya dome ~35 km (KRISP snippet). Use ~30 km normal; 15 km for hot rifts (Afar, N Aegean); 20-25 km for ancient cratonic with rifting.
5. Stretch factors: Hinsken 2007 DL 4-5 km with width 35-60+ km gives a stretching factor from L0/Lf (not stated numerically). Speed 1979 / Basin and Range total extension ~100% median (snippets; primary unverified); Snow & Wernicke ~80% of E-W elongation from thinning (snippet). Afar early stretching factors low due to magma intrusion (Bastow & Keir 2011). Corinth ~1 cm/yr geodetic and 1 to 10 mm/yr late jump at 1 Ma (Armijo 2003).
6. Extension rates: Corinth 1 to 10 mm/yr (Armijo 2003); Corinth ~1 cm/yr over 100 yr (Tiberi snippet); Western Rift "a few mm per year" (Delvaux 2016); Erta Ale figure 16.4 mm/yr (Bastow & Keir 2011 figure label, meaning unverified); Baikal 4 mm/yr vs 2 cm/yr (snippets, disagree); Aegean arc migration 3 cm/yr (Jolivet 2013, not extension); Danakil basin up to ~3 km fill since ~5 Ma (Bastow & Keir).
7. Relief and flank/shoulder heights: Ethiopian MER border escarpments up to ~1500 m (Guraghe), typical local relief ~1000 m (Corti 2018); Livingstone escarpment 2 km (Delvaux 2016); Baikal shoulders 1500-2000 m uplift during fast rifting (Logachev 1993); Western Rift flank 2 km above 1.3 km plateau (Ebinger snippet); Saudi Great Escarpment crest 2150-2400 m, ~4 km total uplift (snippets); flexural uplift Red Sea 500-1200 m (snippet). Basin and Range 1-2 km ridge relief: NOT confirmed.
8. Depths and fill: Baikal 1637 m (Logachev) vs 1642 m (wiki); sediment max 7.5-8 km Baikal, 500-1000 m at edges; Tanganyika 1470 m (FAO) vs 1433 m (Delvaux): disagreement; Dead Sea Lisan 8-8.5 km (USGS snippet); Corinth up to ~3 km syn-rift and 860 m water depth; Rio Grande basins 2-4 km (Averill 2007 via GSA Today 2021). Use lake depth 1450 ± 30 m (Tanganyika) and Baikal 1640 m as rough.
9. Core complexes: MCC dome size: Bossost dome 35 x 15 km (secondary); Damara domes 10x20 to 10x40 km (Oliver snippet); Northern Snake Range extension >=36 km (snippet). Detachment displacements 50-100 km in Aegean (EGU 2024 snippet). MCC spacing 50-200 km as above.
10. Half-graben: Corti 2018 border faults >=50 km long, offset >500 m typical, 2-5 km throws (Ebinger snippet); Permo-Triassic throws 4-5 km (Faerseth 1995); half-graben length 70-190 km for Baikal side basins (Logachev 1993). Displacement/length ratio 0.001-0.1 (snippet, unverified).
11. Failed rifts: MCR ~3000 km long, >2 million km3 volcanics, ~1.1 Ga; SOA/RR inverted (up to 15 km inversion in SOA); Benue width 40 to 250 km depending on source (snippets; primary unverified), Oslo 40-70 km wide, 115 km each side of Oslo (snippet).
12. Passive margin shoulders (pointer only): Red Sea and Afar margins show kilometre-high escarpments with flexural uplift (Red Sea 500-1200 m flexural; Saudi escarpment up to 3 km); further work needed for passive margin brief.
13. Weak spots that need a second pass: Basin and Range range spacing 25-35 km and ridge relief 1-2 km; Kenya dome elevation; Benkhelil Benue dims; Dickinson 2002 IGR; Buck 1991 and Lavier 2000 numbers; Stockli/Wernicke core complex dimensions; brittle layer to graben width scaling.
