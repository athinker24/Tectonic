## 2. Findings by topic

### 2.1 Trench depth and width
- Mariana: max depth 10,935 +/- 6 m; length about 2,550 km; max width 69 km; repeat measurements 10,902 to 10,916 m (1960s to 1996 ROV). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Trench [S].
- Tonga: Horizon Deep 10,800 +/- 10 m, second deepest; described as the fastest plate velocity on Earth (claim not checked). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonga_Trench [S].
- Japan: 8,046 m max; 2011 rupture about 450 km long and 150 km wide (earthquake, not trench width). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Trench [S].
- Peru-Chile: 8,065 m max; the page gives a length of "approximately 5,900 km" and "mean width 64 km" and an area of 590,000 km2. The 5,900 km length looks high and is [U]. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru%E2%80%93Chile_Trench [S].
- Sunda: 3,200 km long; max 7,290 m (Wikipedia) versus 7,192 m +/- 13 m measured by Five Deeps (CTD); these conflict, use about 7.2 to 7.3 km. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Trench [S].
- Aleutian: 3,400 km long; north wall slope 3 to 4 deg; deepest 7,822 m. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Trench [S].
- Trench width: no primary trench-width table was read in this session. Use the Peru-Chile page only with the caveat above. [U] for general width.

### 2.2 Trench to arc distance and forearc
- Stern (2002) gives the forearc (trench to magmatic front) as 166 +/- 60 km wide, citing Gill (1981). Source: https://www.tectonics.caltech.edu/meetings/brownbag/files/2006/SternSubdct2002.pdf [P], section 6.1.
- Stern (2002) quotes arc volcano to seismic zone depth as 124 +/- 38 km and states the relation "does not vary systematically with any subduction variable, such as convergence rate or age of lithosphere". Same PDF [P].
- Jarrard (1986) gives empirical relations for arc-trench gap and slab dip (abstract summary only; full text not read). Source: https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1986RvGeo..24..217J/abstract [X]. A later thesis reports arc gap vs subduction angle R^2 = 0.77 [U].
- Lesser Antilles and Izu-Bonin arc-trench distances from a slab-parameter table (about 213 to 238 km; column alignment uncertain) [U]. Zenodo table: https://zenodo.org/records/8309731/files/Table2_Syracuse2010_updated.pdf (not read; URL returned HTML).

### 2.3 Accretionary prism and forearc basin
- Stern (2002): accretionary and non-accretionary margins are roughly equal in total length; about 80 percent of trench sediment is not accreted (von Huene and Scholl). Accretion forearcs form where sediment supply is high, near continents; thickness above about 400 to 1000 m matters. Source: Stern PDF section 6.1 [P].
- Prism width and thickness numbers: NOT found in the sources read [U]. Jarrard (1986) links shallow dip to prism width (summary) [X].
- Mariana forearc: serpentine mud volcanoes about 2 km high and 30 km diameter. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forearc [S].
- Wedge geometry controlled by pre-orogenic sediment thickness and pore pressure (critical taper). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accretionary_wedge [S]. No km widths taken from it.

### 2.4 Arc position vs slab depth
- Syracuse and Abers (2006), Geochemistry Geophysics Geosystems 7, Q05017, doi 10.1029/2005GC001045: depth to slab beneath arcs averaged over 500 km segments 72 to 173 km, global mean 105 km, plus about 20 km with hypocentral error. Source: https://zendy.io/title/10.1029/2005gc001045 [X] (abstract via search). Full text not read: the Durham and core.ac.uk copies did not return PDFs. Syracuse and Abers (2009) say hypocentre biases can change depth estimates by up to 50 km [X].
- England, Engdahl and Thatcher (2004), Geophysical Journal International 156(2):377-408, doi 10.1111/j.1365-246X.2003.02132.x: depths to tops of intermediate-depth seismicity under arc volcanoes, from the Engdahl et al. catalogue. Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70027074 [metadata P]; method from search summary [X]. Numeric results NOT read.
- CORRECTION: an earlier query tied England et al. (2004) to elevation and crustal thickness. This is wrong. England et al. 2004 is about slab depth, not elevation.
- Stern (2002): 124 +/- 38 km magmatic front above the seismic zone (Gill 1981), interpreted as the depth at which the slab must lie for hot asthenosphere to melt with slab water. Same PDF [P].
- Owner's ~100 km slab depth is consistent with the Syracuse and Abers global mean of 105 km (range 72 to 173 km). The 124 km Stern figure is for the magmatic front, not the slab depth itself.

### 2.5 Volcano spacing and arc width
- Stern (2002): "There does not seem to be a characteristic spacing between arc volcanoes (d'Ars et al. 1995)." Candidate controls: upper plate lithosphere thickness (Vogt 1974), gravitational instability in the melt source (Marsh 1979), asthenospheric hot fingers (Tamura et al. 2002). Source: Stern PDF [P].
- Tamura et al. 2002 hot-finger model for NE Japan: inclined thermal fingers about 50 km wide; about ten fingers under Quaternary volcano groups; volcano spacing is used as evidence. Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2019.00040/full (review summary) [X].
- Izu-Bonin volcanic front spacing: regular interval about 60 km (and about 20 and 50 km) in the northern segment; no regular interval in the central segment. Source: https://www2.jpgu.org/meeting/2002/pdf/k080/k080-002_e.pdf (abstract) [X].
- Hasenaka and Carmichael spacing values: NOT found [U].
- Conclusion for the tool: a 30 to 70 km spacing is NOT supported as a universal rule by the sources read. Use it only as a local, segment-scale pattern (20 to 60 km), with irregularity. Treat the owner's 30 to 70 km as [U].
- Arc width (forearc plus arc plus back-arc) numbers beyond the forearc 166 km were not found [U].

### 2.6 Volcano relief and heights
- Nevados Ojos del Salado: highest volcano above sea level, but only about 2,000 m above its base. Source: Smithsonian GVP FAQ text (search summary) https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090808031316/http:/www.volcano.si.edu/faq/index.cfm?faq=04 [X]. The same source warns that base-level errors exceed 100 m for many edifices.
- Andean plateau: about 4 km elevation from the owner's brief, not confirmed in this session [U] (the crust numbers below are consistent with it, not a direct source).
- Kamchatka and Cascades relief above base: NOT found [U].
- Japan stratovolcano relief: NOT found [U].

### 2.7 Andean-type margin crust
- Beck and Zandt (2002) receiver functions: crust under western and eastern cordilleras about 70 to 74 km; thins to 32 to 38 km in the Chaco Plain; central Altiplano at 20S about 60 to 65 km. Source: https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/crustal-thickness-variations-in-the-central-andes-2/ [X].
- Zandt et al. (1996): Bolivian Altiplano crust 65 km from broadband waveforms, Poisson ratio 0.25, interpreted as shortening thickening of a weak felsic layer. Source: https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/anomalous-crust-of-the-bolivian-altiplano-central-andes-constrain-2/ [X].
- Ryan et al. (2016): Moho about 65 km central Altiplano to about 75 km north; 65 to 70 km SE Eastern Cordillera near 20S. [X] (summary only).
- McGlashan, Brown and Kay (2008): 59 to 70 km southern Peru; up to 80 km elsewhere. [X] (summary only).
- Yuan, Sobolev and Kind (2002): Moho about 35 km in the forearc to above 70 km under the plateau. [X] (summary only).
- Stern (2002): Andean-type margins "may be up to 70 km thick". [P]
- Chulick and Mooney: NOT found.

### 2.8 Back-arc basins and spreading
- Sea of Japan: area about 1,050,000 km2; mean depth 1,752 m; max 4,568 m; Japan Basin oceanic and deepest. Japan Arc began forming in the Early Miocene and the sea opened in the Early Miocene. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan [S]. Basin width number not found [U].
- Tyrrhenian Sea: area 275,000 km2; max depth 3,785 m; back-arc from rollback of the Calabrian slab SE in the Neogene; Vavilov and Marsili basins formed in fast and slow retreat episodes. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrrhenian_Sea [S]. Width not found [U].
- Mariana Trough: southern full spreading rate 45 to 64 km/Myr; GPS 15.9 +/- 6.6 km/Myr at 18.7N and 44.6 +/- 2.7 km/Myr at 13.6N; rate rises southward; western flank 2 to 3 times faster in the central trough. Sources: search summaries of JpGU 2025 and related GPS and Geosphere papers [X]; Wikipedia Back-arc basin [S] for the 2 to 3 times asymmetry. Basin width about 100 km (rifted terrain across the northern basin) from a search summary [X].
- Back-arc spreading conditions: subducting crust >= ~55 Myr old; slab dip >30 deg (Wikipedia [S]); Lallemand et al. (2005) summary: spreading where deep dip >~50 deg, shortening where <~30 deg (deeper than 125 km) [X]. The two dip thresholds differ; keep both as ranges.
- Upper-plate motion controls slab dip more than slab pull, age, convergence rate or polarity (Lallemand et al. 2005 summary) [X]. Empirical neutral-subduction relation Vup = 2.5 - 0.5 Vsub (cm/yr) from EGU 2007 abstract [X]. Source: https://meetings.copernicus.org/www.cosis.net/abstracts/EGU2007/04318/EGU2007-J-04318.pdf.
- Rollback: Tyrrhenian is a rollback basin (Wikipedia [S]). Stern (2002) describes a steeper plate and trench rollback in the Mariana-type setting [P] (plate-hinge discussion).
- Arc lifetime and magma evolution: 40 to 100 Myr; boninite to tholeiite to calc-alkaline to shoshonite (Stern 2002 [P]).

### 2.9 Arc magmatic addition rates
- Dimalanta et al. (2002), EPSL 202(1):105-115, doi 10.1016/S0012-821X(02)00761-6: arc crust volume (crust 20 to 30 km) divided by the age at onset of subduction gives 30 to 95 km3 per km of arc per Myr, "nearly twice" earlier estimates. Source: https://repository.geologyscience.ru/handle/123456789/28208 [X]. This is the origin of the owner's 30 to 95 range. It is NOT Jicha and Jagoutz (2015).
- Reymer and Schubert (1984), Tectonics 3:63-77: seismic profiles through 17 arcs give 20 to 40 km3/km/Myr; global 1.65 km3/yr. Source: https://zendy.io/title/10.1029/tc003i001p00063 [X].
- Parcutela et al. (2023), Frontiers in Earth Science: western Pacific arcs 28 to 60 km3/km/Myr from gravity-derived crust. Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1107833/full [X].
- Jicha and Jagoutz (2015): summary says revised intraoceanic output is comparable to slow-to-moderate spreading ridges; numeric values not confirmed. Source: https://www.elementsmagazine.org/author/brian-r-jicha/ [X]. Use only as a qualitative check.
- Ducea et al. (2017) Geology: field-based 300 to 400 km3/km/Myr for a continental arc section, not comparable to the above. Source: https://physpet.ess.washington.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2017/04/Geology-2017-Ducea-G38726.1.pdf [X].
- Disagreement: 20 to 40 (Reymer and Schubert) vs 30 to 95 (Dimalanta) vs 28 to 60 (Parcutela) vs 300 to 400 (continental field section). Factor of about 2 to 3 disagreement among island-arc values, and the 300 to 400 value is a different measure. For the tool, use 20 to 95 km3/km/Myr for intraoceanic arcs, with the upper end as the more recent estimate.

### 2.10 Arc geometry, curvature and oblique convergence
- Frank (1968), "Curvature of Island Arcs", Nature 220:363: correct venue is Nature (owner's brief said JGR). Model: equal-sphere intersection of the arc with a spherical shell. Source: https://scilit.net/publications/046d54ee5da8aa8b671ebb73b59c9377 [metadata; model from search summary X]. Radius-of-curvature numbers: NOT found [U].
- Lesser Antilles: arc about 850 km long; radius of curvature about 450 km. Source: Carleton University course page (teaching, approximate) https://hoopermuseum.carleton.ca/cruise/tectonics/presentday.html [S].
- Aleutian and Izu-Bonin arc radii of curvature: NOT found [U].
- Sumatra: McCaffrey et al. (2000) GPS: oblique convergence split between forearc shortening and trench-parallel shear on the Sumatran fault; fault slip about one-third less than the full margin-parallel component; missing shear seaward of the network. Source: search summary, linked from https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017JGRB..122..572B [X].
- Bradley et al. (2017), JGR 122:572: forearc sliver deformation minor; Sumatran Fault a major strike-slip boundary; partitioning from diffuse deformation in the Wharton Basin. Source: https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017JGRB..122..572B [X]. This disagrees with the 2000 forearc-sliver picture.
- Oblique convergence beneath western Sunda: 43 to 60 mm/yr (garbled preprint snippet) [U].
- McCaffrey (1992) and Sumatran fault slip rate in mm/yr: NOT found [U].

### 2.11 Subduction dip angles and flat slabs
- Stern (2002): the subducted plate "commonly sinks at a steeper angle than the dip of the" hinge. Numeric dip range not in the passages read [P].
- Jarrard (1986) (abstract summary): shallow dip linked to overriding-plate cross-section shape, controlled by prism width and subduction duration [X]. Full text not read; the PDF copy was denied by the tool layer.
- Back-arc spreading dip >30 deg (Wikipedia [S]); Lallemand et al. (2005) >~50 deg and <~30 deg thresholds [X].
- Flat slab (Gutscher et al. 2000, Tectonics 19): Peru flat slab about 1,500 km long; flat subduction at about 10 percent of margins (secondary); upper-plate seismic energy 250 to 800 km from trench several times higher above flat segments. Source: https://zendy.io/title/10.1029/1999tc001152 [X].
- Chile-Argentina flat segment about 28 to 33 S; Peru flat slab north part held by Inca Plateau subduction; southern limit near Nazca Ridge; tomography suggests slab at 200 to 600 km depth is steeper beneath flat segments. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.4040 (preprint, search summary) [X].
- Manea et al. (2017), Tectonophysics, flat slabs in Mexico, Peru, Chile: no single set of conditions explains flattening. [X] (search summary).
- Pampean flat-slab dip angle: NOT found [U]. Flat slab dip numbers (typically near-horizontal, 0 to 10 deg in the flat segment) are not confirmed here.

## Key takeaways for the tool
1. Trench depth: use 6 to 8 km typical, with 10.8 to 10.9 km as the deepest case (Tonga, Mariana). Sunda and Peru-Chile values are around 7.2 to 8.1 km. Trench width was not established from a primary source.
2. Trench to arc distance: about 166 km forearc (+/- 60 km), from Stern (2002) citing Gill (1981). Derived working range (166 +/- 60 km, so about 106 to 226 km), not a single measured value per arc.
3. Arc position vs slab depth: magmatic front about 124 km (+/- 38) above the seismic zone (Stern 2002); global mean slab depth beneath arcs 105 km, range 72 to 173 km (Syracuse and Abers 2006, abstract). Place arcs where slab depth is about 100 to 120 km.
4. Volcano spacing: NOT a fixed 30 to 70 km. Stern (2002) states there is no characteristic spacing. Use irregular spacing with local regular segments of about 20 to 60 km. Treat the owner's number as an assumption.
5. Arc width: forearc 166 km; magmatic arc itself and back-arc width not established. Use Wikipedia back-arc basins (a few hundred km wide at most) for back-arc.
6. Andean crust: 65 km central plateau, 70 to 74 km cordilleras, 80 km upper bound. Plateau elevation of about 4 km is not verified here.
7. Volcano relief: stratovolcanoes rise about 2 km above their base in the Andes (Ojos del Salado). Kamchatka and Cascades relief not found; no source-backed value. Any 1 to 2.5 km planning range would be the author's guess, not a sourced number.
8. Back-arc spreading: needs slab older than about 55 Myr and dip above about 30 deg; deep dip above about 50 deg favours spreading, below about 30 deg favours shortening (Lallemand et al. 2005, summary). Mariana Trough full spreading rate 15 to 64 km/Myr; Tyrrhenian max depth 3.8 km; Sea of Japan max depth 4.6 km.
9. Arc magmatic addition: 20 to 95 km3/km/Myr; the 30 to 95 range is Dimalanta et al. (2002), not Jicha and Jagoutz (2015). Use 30 to 60 as a central band.
10. Arc geometry: Lesser Antilles about 850 km long with about 450 km radius of curvature (approximate). Frank (1968) radius numbers not found; a spherical-shell model is the framework (Nature, not JGR).
11. Oblique convergence: Sumatra partitions oblique convergence between forearc shortening and a trench-parallel fault; the fault takes about two-thirds of the margin-parallel motion (McCaffrey et al. 2000 summary); a 2017 study disagrees about the forearc sliver. Use partitioning with uncertainty.
12. Flat slabs: about 10 percent of margins (Gutscher et al. 2000 summary); Peru flat slab about 1,500 km; Chile-Argentina flat 28 to 33 S. Dip angles of flat segments not confirmed.
13. Accretionary prisms: accretion depends on sediment thickness (over about 400 to 1,000 m) and proximity to continents; prism widths not established.
14. Gaps still open: Syracuse and Abers full text, England et al. 2004 numbers, Frank 1968 radius, Jarrard 1986 regressions, Lallemand 2005 full text, trench width values, Kamchatka and Cascades relief, Sumatra fault slip rate.
