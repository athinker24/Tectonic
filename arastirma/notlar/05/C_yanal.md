## 1. San Andreas Fault (SAF)

## 2. North Anatolian Fault (NAF), Sea of Marmara

- Marmara pull-apart geometry: "smaller strike-slip segments and pull-apart basins alternate within the main step-over." Armijo et al. 2005, G-Cubed 6(6) Q06009. Source read (PDF): https://www.ipgp.fr/~armijo/ArmijoPDF/ArmijoG-Cubed05.pdf
  - Central basin lozenge-shaped pull-apart between two strike-slip segments, bounded by normal scarps. Same source.
  - Sedimentation 1-3 mm/yr vs normal faulting up to 6 mm/yr at pull-apart margins. Same source.
  - 1912 Ganos rupture: about 140 km total from Gulf of Saros to Marmara, right-lateral slip 5 m, crossed Ganos restraining bend. Same source.
  - Segment 70 km long between Cinarcik and Central basins, Coulomb slip deficit 4-5 m. Same source.
- GPS: right-lateral 18-20 mm/yr plus extension 8 mm/yr across northern Marmara basin (Flerit et al. 2003; Armijo et al. 2003, as quoted in Armijo 2005). Same source.
- Geological rates on the northern strand about 10 mm/yr (search snippet, UNVERIFIED: https://nosamsresearch.whoi.edu/geological-slip-rates-along-north-anatolian-fault-marmara-region).
- Westward propagation / Anatolian escape: qualitative only so far (search snippet, UNVERIFIED primary).

## 5. Pull-apart basin scaling (read: Gurbuz 2010 review)

Source read (PDF, Geosphere, copy at https://people.uncw.edu/lamaskint/GLY%20445-545%20FALL%202013/Gurbuz%202010%20GEOSPHERE%20Pull%20Apart%20Basins.pdf): Gurbuz, A. 2010, "Geometric characteristics of pull-apart basins", Geosphere. The following are as reported in that review.
- Aspect ratio 3:1 (length:width) in 2-D, the "well known" rule; most directly measured ratios 3 to 4. Same source.
- Aydin and Nur 1982 fit to natural basins: log l = c1 log w + log c2, c1 approx 1, 2.4 < c2 < 4.3, mean c2 = 3.2. Gurbuz calls the dataset 62 active basins, while the Aydin and Nur 1982 Tectonics abstract (see below) describes 70 rhomb-like basins and pressure ridges from the western US. Discrepancy in sample count, UNVERIFIED which is right. Same source.
- Basin width fixed by width of the releasing bend, length grows with overlap and displacement. "Basin width does not increase significantly and remains fixed by the width of the releasing bend." Mann et al. 1983 idea: rectangular form from lengthening S- or Z-shaped basin with increased master-fault overlap. Same source.
- Aydin and Nur 1982 abstract (Tectonics 1, 91-105, doi:10.1029/tc001i001p00091): width of rhombs controlled by initial fault geometry, length increases with displacement; widening by coalescence and new strands. Source (abstract fragments only, read via search snippets): https://zendy.io/title/10.1029/tc001i001p00091 (partial). Scilit page was access-denied (see failed fetches).
- Acute angle of bounding faults: 30-35 deg (mean 33 deg from Bahat 1983 and others). Bertoluzza and Perotti 1997: a few degrees in transtension up to 100-120 deg in transpression. Same source.
- Depth: Rodgers 1980: when master-fault overlap > 2x separation, basin depth ~10% of displacement along master faults. Same source.
- Depth vs length: Hempton and Dunne 1984, true deposit thickness d = 0.8 l + 0.26 (units in km, UNVERIFIED units in the review). Same source.
- Gurbuz regression from 11 basins: d = 0.1104 l - 0.08755 w (his Eq. 4, units not stated in extract). Same source.
- Dead Sea Basin: 130 km long, 18 km wide, l/w 7.2 (Gurbuz, citing Zak and Freund 1981). Three subbasins with l/w 2.4, 3.3, 2.6 (Basile and Brun 1999 as cited). Same source.
- Anatolian NAF basins (Erzincan, Susehri, Niksar, Tasova-Erbaa, Yenicaga, Bolu, Pamukova, Yenisehir, Cinarcik, Central, Tekirdag) have curved boundaries on the eastern half of NAF; Bahat 1983 says curved boundaries need no horizontal compression; attributed to westward Anatolian motion relaxing the eastern half and compressing the western half (Bellier et al. 1997 as cited). Same source.
- Basin length/width vs depth: 'Most pull-apart basins have low length-to-width ratios' (short lives). Same source.

## 7. Salton Trough / Salton Sea (read primary)

Source read (PDF copy): https://igppweb.ucsd.edu/~dkilb/NG/SaltonSea/Publications_files/brothers_NG_2009.pdf , Brothers et al. 2009, Nature Geoscience, doi:10.1038/NGEO590, "Tectonic evolution of the Salton Sea inferred from seismic reflection data."
- Pull-apart in the SAF-Imperial fault transtensional step-over; northward transition from oblique divergent Gulf of California margin to transpression of SAF. Same source.
- Hinge zone (Extra fault zone, NE-trending) splits the sea into northern and southern sub-basins. Same source.
- Differential subsidence in southern sub-basin >10 mm/yr; average 9-12 mm/yr south of hinge; ~19 mm/yr in southern basin since ~AD 1360 (as stated in text). Same source.
- Onset of extension in Salton Sea after ~0.5 Ma; slip partitioned spatially and temporally into vertical and horizontal domains. Same source.
- Palaeoearthquakes with >1 m vertical offset per event (Supplement reference). Same source.
- Slip-rate context in the paper: long-term rate from offset alluvial fans north of Salton Sea 9-15 mm/yr over 45-50 kyr; short-term geodetic 23.3 +/- 0.5 mm/yr; 75-100% of horizontal slip transferred from SAF into Salton Sea accommodated by extension (as stated; context partly checked). Same source.
- Brawley Seismic Zone: <5 km wide dextral shear zone with block rotation (Brawley zone; search snippet of Salton Trough sources, UNVERIFIED primary for width). Brothers et al. 2009 text mentions "narrow (<5-km-wide), dextral shear zone" for BSZ (same source, line ~223).
- Salton Trough Wikipedia snippet: ~185 km long NW-SE, sediments locally >6 km (UNVERIFIED primary; Wikipedia only).
- Elmore Ranch 1987 (USGS, snippet): M6.2, principal rupture up to 9 km, surface slip max 12.5 cm; Wikipedia gives 20-25 km length (inconsistent; UNVERIFIED primary).
- Basin width of Salton pull-apart: not found in sources read.

## 8. Sumatran Fault (read primary)

Source read (PDF): https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20000064068/downloads/20000064068.pdf , Prawirodirdjo, Bock, Genrich, Puntodewo, Rais, Subarya, Sutisna, 2000, JGR (in press), "One century of tectonic deformation along the Sumatran Fault from triangulation and GPS surveys."
- Central Sumatran Fault arc-parallel velocity consistent with slip rates 23 to 24 mm/yr (GPS). Same source (abstract).
- Locking depth ~20 km between 1.0 S and 1.3 N; large (Mw~7) earthquakes. Same source.
- Forearc sliver not rigid, arc-parallel stretching requires NW increase in slip rate along the SF; GPS shows only marginal increase, earlier stream offset and SPOT studies (Sieh et al. 1991; Bellier and Sebrier 1995) support SE-to-NW increase. Same source.
- Slip on SF about 1/3 less than full arc-parallel plate component (McCaffrey et al., this volume); additional ~20 mm/yr strike-slip west of SF, probably seaward of Mentawai islands. Same source.
- 20 km of offset on SF since the Oligocene (Sieh and Natawidjaja, this volume, as cited). Same source.
- Plate convergence 52-60 mm/yr (azimuth N7-17E, as in the paper's text). Same source.
- Snippet-level (UNVERIFIED primary): Sieh and Natawidjaja 2000: 19-20 segments; slip 5 mm/yr near Sunda Strait to 27 mm/yr near Toba; 11-27 mm/yr (Caltech). Discontinuity widths 4-12 km (snippet).
- Sagaing (snippets, UNVERIFIED primary): length ~1200-1400 km; 203 km offset from 1991 study (slip ~22 mm/yr); 1930 rupture ~460 km; GNSS 20-23 mm/yr; overall fault slip 11-24 mm/yr; plate-boundary 35 mm/yr with 18 mm/yr on Sagaing (snippet).

## 11. Alpine Fault (read primary: McGrath et al. 2025)

Source read (PDF, open): McGrath, J.D. et al. 2025, JGR Solid Earth, "Linking geodetically resolved uplift to long-term orogenic exhumation in the Southern Alps" : https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/235658/1/JGR%20Solid%20Earth%20-%202025%20-%20McGrath%20-%20Linking%20Geodetically%20Resolved%20Uplift%20to%20Long%E2%80%90Term%20Orogenic%20Exhumation%20in%20the%20Southern.pdf
- Relative Pacific-plate motion ~38 to 40 mm/yr across the boundary; ~30% (~11-12 mm/yr) taken up by internal deformation of the Southern Alps; remaining ~70% (~27 mm/yr) on the Alpine Fault. Same source (intro, and "27 mm/yr" with Norris and Cooper 2001 cited).
- Dextral slip on the Alpine Fault began early Miocene; convergence rate increase at ~6 Ma caused uplift and exhumation. Same source.
- Central Alpine Fault: great (Mw > 8) earthquakes; last 1717; recurrence 249-329 yr. Same source.
- Fault dip ~48 +/- 7 deg; locking depth 10 +/- 1.5 km; fault to 35-40 km depth (seismic reflection, Stern et al. 2007 as cited). Same source.
- Along-strike strike-slip rate from GNSS model: 24-25 mm/yr north and centre, 17 mm/yr south; peak dip-slip 8 +/- 1 mm/yr in central profile (>50% above 5 mm/yr to north and south). Same source.
- Uplift: GNSS uplift 6-12 mm/yr focused in 50 x 30 km around Aoraki/Mt Cook, highest peak 3724 m in Southern Alps. Same source. Peak erosion/exhumation/uplift rates 7-13 mm/yr (as cited, same source line ~177).
- Snippet-level (UNVERIFIED primary): Barrell and Cox 2003 field guide: 27 +/- 5 mm/yr, 70-75% of plate motion (search snippet, https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/235658 partly). Late Cenozoic mean rock uplift max ~2.8 mm/yr at the fault, ~1.0 mm/yr in east (snippet).
- Total offset 410-480 km: not confirmed in the McGrath paper text (grep found no total offset statement). Still UNVERIFIED primary for the 470 km figure; snippet range 410-480 km.
- Disagreement: GNSS-based dip-slip after unloading correction ~4-6 mm/yr (Liu et al. 2024 as cited, snippet).

## 19. Key takeaways for the tool
(These are my synthesis of sourced numbers. Confidence noted.)
1. Pull-apart basins: plan-view length:width ~3:1 (Aydin and Nur 1982 log-linear fit with slope ~1, mean intercept c2 = 3.2, range 2.4-4.3; Gurbuz 2010 citing). Width is set by releasing-bend width; length grows with overlap and displacement. Acute bounding angles ~30-35 deg. Use: basin length L = 3.2 * W (range 2.4-4.3 W), W tied to stepover width. Source read: Gurbuz 2010 and Armijo 2005.
2. Pull-apart depth: Rodgers 1980 (via Gurbuz): depth ~10% of displacement when overlap > 2x separation. Hempton and Dunne 1984 (via Gurbuz): deposit thickness d = 0.8 L + 0.26 (units to check). Dead Sea scale: 130 km x 18 km, depths 8-16 km by method.
3. Real examples: Marmara central basin lozenge with normal scarps; Salton Sea subsiding 9-19 mm/yr; onset ~0.5 Ma (Brothers 2009).
4. Fault length vs offset: the SAF and NAF show offsets far below length-based expectations; NAF total offset 80-85 km along ~1000-1500 km length (Hubert-Ferrari 2002 and endpoints). Ratio is about 0.05-0.1 offset/length for NAF; not computed for SAF. Length choice dominates the ratio. Source: Hubert-Ferrari 2002 (read); length values snippet-level.
5. Segmentation: Marmara 70 km segment (Armijo 2005); Sumatra 19-20 segments with 4-12 km discontinuities (snippet). Use segment lengths 20-150 km with releasing/restraining steps 4-12 km wide for discontinuities (snippet, verify).
6. Slip rates (mm/yr) to use as ranges: SAF 24-35 (Cajon 24.5, Carrizo 34); NAF 18-22 (Holocene and GPS; Neogene 6.5); Alpine 27 (+/-5); Sagaing 11-24 (snippet); Sumatra 11-27 geologic, 23-24 GPS; Altyn Tagh 8-12 (snippet); EAF 6-10 (snippet); Karakoram 3-32 (disputed).
7. Cumulative offsets (km): SAF 160-320; NAF 65-100; Alpine 410-480; DST ~105; Altyn Tagh ~360 (Qaidam piercing, snippet); Sagaing ~203 (snippet); Karakoram <120 (snippet).
8. Strike-slip topography: linear, narrow zones of uplift at restraining bends and valleys at releasing bends. Pop-up examples: Altun Shan up to 5830 m (snippet, Altyn Tagh restraining bend); Alpine Fault peak 3724 m with uplift 50 x 30 km focus (McGrath 2025). Collision belt relief is broader; a 1-2 km benchmark was not confirmed.
9. Shutter ridges: offset length set mostly by shutter ridge length (Harbert 2018 snippet), not total offset. Channels offset 5-35 m per event at Carrizo (Liu-Zeng 2006 read: 5-8 m events, 1.4 m low end).
10. Strain partitioning: oblique margins partition into trench-parallel strike-slip plus forearc sliver (Sumatra; Alpine Fault 70% of plate motion on fault, 30% in Southern Alps; Armijo 2005 Marmara shows asymmetric partitioning). Use a partition fraction as a parameter: Alpine 0.7 on fault, Marmara extension plus strike-slip.
11. Fault zone width and damage: no firm km-scale width law confirmed; Mitchell and Faulkner 2009 and Faulkner 2011 give damage-zone widths scaling with displacement (snippet). Keep width as a parameter (SAF/Brawley zone <5 km wide per Brothers 2009).
12. Gaps to fix before final tool design: primary text for Sylvester 1988, Mann 2007, Harbert 2018, Savage and Brodsky 2011, Dewey and Jones 1998, Wallace 1968, Dead Sea DESIRE, Altyn Tagh primary, NAF valley width.
