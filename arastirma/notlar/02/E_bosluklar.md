## Gap 3 and 5 (Mongolia, partial): summit plateaus and valley elevations
- Gobi Altay and Altay flat-topped massifs are "~4000 m high" with flat summit plateaus, interpreted as uplifted parts of a Jurassic peneplain; the Valley of Lakes is a "~1000-m-high flat plain" running from the Gobi Desert to the Siberian border. Source: Jolivet et al. 2007, Geology, https://www.isterre.fr/IMG/pdf/jolivet_et_al_geology_2007.pdf
- Ih Bogd summit plateau (sample IB-05-4) at "Altitude: 3600 m"; plateau ~20 km long, max ~8 km wide. Source: https://www.isterre.fr/IMG/pdf/jolivet_et_al_geology_2007.pdf
- Uplift of these surfaces began "ca. 5 +/- 3 Ma" per the abstract. Source: https://www.isterre.fr/IMG/pdf/jolivet_et_al_geology_2007.pdf

## Gap 6: Zagros taper and basal dip (primary: Mouthereau, Lacombe & Meyer 2006)
- Fars province is an arcuate folded area "200 km wide". Source: Mouthereau et al. 2006, Geophys. J. Int. 165, 336-356, https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/2006GeoJI.165..336M (PDF text read; ADS URL gave a PDF; the same paper is also listed at the search hit above).
- Basement dip beta set to 0.5 degrees, from restored sections (McQuarrie 2004); the paper cites a basement dip of 0.5 degrees. Source: Mouthereau et al. 2006 (same PDF).
- Observed average surface slopes across the Zagros folded belt: 0.3 to 0.5 degrees. Source: Mouthereau et al. 2006 (same PDF).
- Frictional (Coulomb) critical wedges are "essentially topographically flat" and cannot fit the observed 0.3-0.5 deg slopes. Source: Mouthereau et al. 2006.
- Best-fit brittle-ductile wedge tapers in the model: 0.9 and 1.36 degrees. Source: Mouthereau et al. 2006.
- Salt-based fold-thrust belts have tapers "<1 degree" (Davis and Engelder 1985 as cited). Source: Mouthereau et al. 2006.
- The Mediterranean Ridge (salt-based accretionary wedge) has a surface angle 0.2-0.3 degrees over a detachment dipping ~1 degree on average (Kukowski et al. 2002 as cited). Source: Mouthereau et al. 2006.
- Pyrenees taper: UNVERIFIED so far (see Meigs and Burbank 1997 below, not yet read).

## Gap 4: Indian plate flexure and elastic thickness (partial)
- Duvall et al. 2021, Basin Research, "Indian plate structural inheritance in the Himalayan foreland basin, Nepal": subsidence controlled by inherited lateral variations in flexural rigidity of the Indian plate. Source: https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/155586/1/Duvall_al2021BRE_006_2021.R2_Proof_hi.pdf
- Foreland basin depth "~5 km" over an across-strike distance of "~200 km" (line 622 of the manuscript). Source: Duvall et al. 2021 (same PDF).
- Average elastic thickness as low as 25-32 km (McKenzie et al. 2014) or higher (Jordan and Watts 2005), as cited in Duvall et al. 2021. Source: Duvall et al. 2021 (same PDF).
- Flexural parameter alpha = (4D/(rho_m - rho_fill)g)^(1/4) with D = E T^3 / (12(1-nu^2)); displacement scales as T^(3/4) etc. Source: Duvall et al. 2021 (same PDF).
