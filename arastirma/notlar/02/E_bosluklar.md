## Gap 1: What makes a small continental fragment high (mechanisms, with sources)
- Taiwan: "accretion due to arc-continent collision has continued since Late Miocene time" and the Central Range is "now 3000-4000 m" and "has risen faster since less than 1 Ma, with tectonic uplift outpacing erosion" [S] EGU24-7340, https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU24/EGU24-7340.html (snippet only, not read).
- Taiwan: "mature arc-continent collision ... mountain belt roughly 4 km high" and mountain belt about 50 km wide in central Taiwan [S], GSA abstract via search, URL not captured in full; thesis source https://www.gep.ncu.edu.tw/storage/thesis/2016/thesis_1448497773.pdf (only a figure caption read [R]).
- Taiwan: crust thickens from about 24 km in the Taiwan Strait to about 40 km beneath the eastern Central Range [S], 2016 GJI paper (URL not captured in full).
- Taiwan: Plio-Pleistocene steady state in the northern two-thirds of the Central Range; topography supported by crustal-scale imbrication, which questions the steady-state critically tapered wedge interpretation [S], search results only.
- Cyprus: Robertson et al. 1995, Terra Nova 7(2) 254-264 [S]: Eratosthenes Seamount is subsiding, breaking up and being thrust beneath Cyprus; northward underthrusting linked to late Pliocene to mid-Quaternary uplift centred on the Troodos ophiolite. Source: search snippet, https://malvernu3a.org.uk/geology3/data-files/old-site/walks/2015/Cyprus/Williams%20Record/Articles/Neotethys.htm (secondary).
- Cyprus: collision timing is contested: 1.5-2 Ma in a 2017 Stockholm thesis [S] (https://www.su.se/polopoly_fs/1.554699.1620635323!/menu/standard/file/2017_hermes-pantazidis_bsc_geology_15hp.pdf); Miocene microcontinent collision in another summary [S] (Nature Index topic page, URL not captured).
- Crete: uplift possibly from wedge inflation by underplated material or asthenospheric wedging by the southward-retreating African slab; forearc extension driven by slab rollback [S], 2012 abstract (URL not captured).
- Timor and Sumba: Australian plate in the Timor Sea moves NNE at 70 mm/yr relative to the Sunda Shelf [R] (Duffy 2017, https://www.geologist.nl/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/2017_Duffy_JAES.pdf, citing Bock et al. 2003 and Koulali et al. 2016). Duffy 2017 says uplift may have been partly driven by a transient imbalance between rock uplift and denudation as resistant lithologies emerged from mélange-like mudstone [R] (same URL). Sumba: area 11,200 km2 and maximum elevation 1209 m [R] (same URL).
- Timor and Sulawesi: Early Miocene collision of the Sula Spur (Australian promontory) with North Sulawesi arc, with Banda subduction rollback opening the North and South Banda basins [S], review snippet (URL not captured).
- South Island: plate convergence taken up mainly by dip-slip on the Alpine Fault at about 12 mm/yr [S], citing Norris and Cooper 2001, in a 2019 Tectonics paper (URL not captured; not read). Adjacent uplift rates 6-9 mm/yr in the central Southern Alps, falling to 5-6 mm/yr NE and about 3 mm/yr south of Haast [S] (same secondary source).
- Corsica and Sardinia: block rotated about 60 deg counterclockwise after 21 Ma, drifting at the rear of a rolling-back Alpine Tethys slab [S], Siravo et al. 2023, Tectonics (https://www.earth-prints.org/bitstream/2122/16463/1/Siravo%20et%20al.%2c%202023_Tectonics_Sardinia_Cixerri.pdf, snippet only). This explains rotation and separation, not elevation.
- No review was found that explains why small fragments are high in general. UNVERIFIED as a general rule.

## Gap 4: Himalayan and other foreland flexure
- India Plate beneath the Ganges basin (Hetenyi et al. 2006, GJI 167(3) 1106-1118, DOI 10.1111/j.1365-246X.2006.03198.x) [R, abstract, https://real.mtak.hu/5945/]:
  - "Receiver functions give a high-resolution image of the base of the foreland basin at similar to 5 km depth", and crust increases from about 35 km under the Indo-Gangetic plain to about 70 km in southern Tibet.
  - "the EET of the India Plate decreases northwards from 60-80 to 20-30 km as it is flexed down beneath Himalaya and Tibet".
  - The most abrupt EET drop is about 200 km south of the MFT, associated with crust-mantle decoupling [S] (search snippet of the full text, https://core.ac.uk/download/11857861.pdf, which returned HTML).
- Duvall et al. 2021, Basin Research (Nepal lowlands) [R, manuscript, https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/155586/1/Duvall_al2021BRE_006_2021.R2_Proof_hi.pdf]:
  - Foreland basin depth of "about 5 km" over an across-strike distance of "about 200 km" (manuscript line 622).
  - About 8 km lateral variation in Vindhyan and Gondwanan sediment thickness; basement ridges around 50 km wide (Delhi-Haridwar ridge).
  - Average EET cited as 25-32 km (McKenzie et al. 2014) or higher (Jordan and Watts 2005), as cited in the manuscript.
  - Subsidence controlled by inherited lateral variations in flexural rigidity of the Indian plate.
- Lyon-Caen and Molnar 1983 and 1985 (Tectonics 4, 513-538): search snippet [S] gives constant flexural rigidity about 0.7e25 N m (range 0.2-2.0e25 N m) for the Lesser Himalaya and Ganga basin, and 0.1-1.0e23 N m beneath the Greater Himalaya. Full text not read. A 100 km EET attributed to 1985 was NOT confirmed.
- Ganges foreland basin width: NOT FOUND. Maximum sediment fill "up to 6 km" in an older cited study [S].
- Po Plain and Adriatic: Kruse and Royden 1994, Tectonics 13(2) 278-302, DOI 10.1029/93TC01935 [R, abstract, https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/geo_facpub/942]:
  - Central Adriatic Te = 5-10 km for Eocene-Oligocene flexure; Te = 10-15 km for Pliocene-Quaternary flexure.
  - Constant Te = 10 km in the central Adriatic and Te = 15 km in the northern Adriatic and Po Plain.
  - Little evidence for viscous relaxation on 2-50 Ma timescales.
  - Flexural wavelength values for the Po Plain: NOT FOUND.
- Mesopotamian foreland (Zagros): Pirouz et al. 2017, GJI 210(3) 1659-1680, DOI as listed at the Caltech record [R, abstract, https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/1bgnp-74m19]:
  - Foreland depth varies from 1 to 6 km along the Zagros strike, deepest beneath the Dezful embayment.
  - "The equivalent elastic thickness of the flexed Arabian lithosphere is estimated to be ca. 50 km."
  - Saura et al. Te rising from about 20 km (Amiran stage) to about 55 km (Mesopotamian stage) [S, author unverified].
  - AGU 2016 abstract (Pirouz et al.): foreland wavelength may carry little information on lithospheric flexural rigidity [S].
- Flexural wavelength values for the Ganges and Mesopotamian foreland: NOT FOUND in a read source. Use Te values above, not wavelengths.

## Gap 6: Taper angles and basal dips
- Zagros (Mouthereau, Lacombe and Meyer 2006, GJI 165, 336-356) [R, full text, https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/2006GeoJI.165..336M]:
  - Fars folded belt "200 km wide".
  - Observed average surface slopes across the Zagros folded belt: 0.3-0.5 deg.
  - Basement dip beta set to 0.5 deg, from restored sections (McQuarrie 2004).
  - Frictional critical wedges "are essentially topographically flat" and cannot fit observed slopes of 0.3-0.5 deg.
  - Best-fit brittle-ductile wedge tapers in the model: 0.9 and 1.36 deg.
  - Salt-based fold-thrust belts: tapers "<1 deg" (Davis and Engelder 1985, as cited).
  - Mediterranean Ridge (salt-based accretionary wedge): surface angle 0.2-0.3 deg over a detachment dipping about 1 deg on average (Kukowski et al. 2002, as cited).
- Pyrenees: Meigs and Burbank 1997, Tectonics 16, DOI 10.1029/96TC03641 [S, abstract only, https://zendy.io/title/10.1029/96tc03641]. Taper controlled by lower-plate flexural rigidity, relative relief creation rate front versus rear, sediment deposition in the wedge, and pretectonic wedge taper. Basal detachment angle decreased overall as the belt moved onto stronger crust. Numerical taper and basal dip values: NOT FOUND (full text not accessed).
- Pyrenees taper in degrees: UNVERIFIED.

## Key takeaways for the tool
1. Use three access paths and check each: helper (`oku.py`) for HTML and Wikipedia; `curl` then `pdftotext -layout` for PDFs; abstracts from institutional repositories (real.mtak.hu, digitalcommons, Caltech) when PDFs are blocked. Many PDF URLs return HTML landing pages (core.ac.uk) and must be re-routed to the repository record.
2. Zenodo (GEO Mountains) returns 403 to the fetch client, so the GEO series cannot be read directly. Record page only.
3. WebSearch snippets often contain plausible numbers that the source does not support (for example, the Hangai 4,000 m and 1.5 km, and a 100 km Te attributed to Lyon-Caen and Molnar 1985). Mark snippet numbers [S] and do not cite them as primary without a read.
4. Encyclopedia values disagree within a single page (Corsica area 8,680 vs 8,722 km2) and across sites (Junggar: Britannica 300-450 m vs HK Education Bureau 500-1,000 m). Report ranges, not a single value.
5. Gaps that remain open and need a targeted next round: (a) mean elevations of Corsica, Sardinia, South Island, Sulawesi, Timor, Crete, Cyprus; (b) Alps swath-profile numbers (try EGU, Tectonics, Swiss Journal of Geosciences, and SRTM or EU-DEM hypsometry papers); (c) Ganges basin width and flexural wavelength (try Lyon-Caen and Molnar 1985 full text from AGU); (d) Pyrenees taper and basal dip (Meigs and Burbank 1997 full text); (e) Mongolian, Tarim and Junggar DEM-based mean elevations; (f) survey-level lengths for the Tian Shan, Pyrenees widths, Caucasus widths.
6. Confirmed facts for the tool, with the strongest sources: Zagros surface slope 0.3-0.5 deg and Fars belt 200 km wide (Mouthereau 2006, read); India Plate EET decreasing from 60-80 km to 20-30 km northwards (Hetenyi 2006, abstract read); Zagros foreland Te about 50 km and foreland depth 1-6 km (Pirouz 2017, abstract read); Po Plain Te 15 km (Kruse and Royden 1994, abstract read); Gobi Altay summit plateaus about 4000 m and the Valley of Lakes about 1000 m (Jolivet 2007, read); Sumba area 11,200 km2 and maximum 1,209 m (Duffy 2017, read).
