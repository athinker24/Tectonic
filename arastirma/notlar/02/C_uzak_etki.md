# C. Far field deformation and rigid blocks (working notes, 2026-11-11)
Convention: every fact is followed by the URL it was read at. UNVERIFIED = not confirmed from a source I read in full. No em/en dashes.

## 1. Tien Shan: distance, lag, rates, reactivation
- Tian Shan lies ~1500 km north of the India-Asia plate boundary; major Cenozoic uplift came >30 Myr after collision onset (Huangfu et al. 2021 GRL, abstract level only, full text not read) https://gdlab.ucas.edu.cn/index.php/en/yjfx/2020-03-27-06-27-26/3077-2021-geophysical-research-letters-2 (via search summary; UNVERIFIED in detail)
- Huangfu et al. 2021 2D thermomechanical models: direct Indian lithospheric mantle vs rigid Tarim lithospheric mantle contact beneath western Tibet dominates Tian Shan building; models vary thermorheology and convergence rate. (search summary only) https://zenodo.org/record/4818316 (dataset), UNVERIFIED
- GPS: N-S shortening across Tianshan at ~76E "at least ~20 mm/yr"; South Tianshan 75-78E convergence 15-24 mm/yr; Pamir-South Tianshan junction takes ~68% of total convergence, ~58% in intermontane faults/basins in north of South Tianshan; deformation attenuates west to east and south to north with distance from Pamir. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2021.793890/full
- Other GPS numbers (from search summary, not verified at source): ~20 mm/yr west of Issyk Kul decreasing to ~9 mm/yr over ~500 km eastward; ~12 mm/yr across northern 2/3 of central Tien Shan; ~8 mm/yr eastern Tian Shan. (search summaries; need primary: Zubovich 2010, Reigber 2001) UNVERIFIED
- Reactivation: Zhao et al. 2025 (Solid Earth 16, 503), Harlik Mountains, easternmost Tianshan: relict low-relief surfaces (AFT ~110-100 Ma) formed after Mongol-Okhotsk collapse; fault zones cool 90-70 Ma; "by the Oligocene (~30 Ma), far-field effects from India-Eurasia collision reactivated major faults in a left-lateral sense, driving uplift, tilting, incision"; Cenozoic exhumation of Harlik <2 km; relict surfaces now at 3000-3500 m (in places); Cenozoic ranges uplifted "mainly along pre-existing faults, resulting in a basin-and-range-type landscape". https://se.copernicus.org/articles/16/503/2025/ and https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2024/egusphere-2024-3668/egusphere-2024-3668-manuscript-version3.pdf
- Takeaway from same paper: Mesozoic low-relief surfaces survive inside active ranges because erosion rates are low; far-field belts inherit old planation surfaces. same URL.

## 2. Pamir indenter and Tarim as rigid block
- Pamir protrudes ~300 km north relative to Tibet, between Tajik basin and cratonic Tarim; Indian mantle indenter underthrusts Tarim along a compressive transform boundary (Bloch et al. 2021 GRL). https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/531322/1/2021GL095413.pdf

## 3. Effective elastic thickness (Te) of rigid blocks
- Te of Tarim basin 66+-7 km from heat flow (Burov 1995 method), larger than crustal thickness 45 km; low-heat-flow blocks Tarim, Junggar, Ordos, Sichuan have mantle dominated strength; seismicity and active faults localize at boundaries of high Te blocks (Tarim, Ordos); Wenchuan EQ at contrast Sichuan block vs weak Tibet margin (Liu and Wang, EGU2009-362 abstract). https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2009/EGU2009-362.pdf
- Other values (search summary only, UNVERIFIED): Braitenberg 2002 Te up to 110 km Tarim, 70 km Qaidam; Tien Shan flexure study Te 40-50 km in Tarim/Junggar/Kazakh to 20-23 km beneath Tien Shan.

## 4. Mongolian plateau (search-level, to be verified)
- Hangay Dome ~200,000 km2 within Mongolian Plateau ~425,000 km2; dome interior locally >4000 m with high-elevation low-relief paleo-erosion surface; basalts 30 Ma to present; uplift recent, cause debated. https://eesarchive.lehigh.edu/groups/mongolia/indepth/hightopography.html

## 7. Qilian, Kunlun, Qaidam timing (search summaries; conflicting)
- South Tian Shan and Qilian rose before West and East Kunlun, linked to far-field stress transfer; South Tian Shan uplift ~26-24 Ma (2020 Tectonophysics modelling study, search summary). UNVERIFIED primary.
- East Kunlun and Laji Shan ~30-22 Ma exhumation; crustal shortening dominant since ~15 Ma (review, search summary). UNVERIFIED.
- Qaidam basin fill onset ~25.5 Ma, continuous to ~4.8 Ma; Qilian detritus only from ~12 Ma (magnetostratigraphy, search summary). Another study: onset ~20 Ma, Qilian provenance after ~18.5 Ma. https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10128969 UNVERIFIED
- Qilian thrust reactivation ca. 16 Ma (thermochronology, search summary); another Qilian study: two-phase Eocene-Early Miocene then change ~8 +/-1 Ma. Onset estimates for Qilian-Nan Shan range from early Paleocene to late Miocene (one study). UNVERIFIED

## 8. Altai: rates and onset (primary text read unless marked)
- Ramel et al. 2025 EGUsphere preprint (full text read; preprint, not peer reviewed): first block model of Mongolian Altay; ~4-6 mm/yr dextral strike-slip across ~400 km-wide Altay deformation zone; onset of Altay massif formation "between 20 and 5 million years ago" linked to transpressive reactivation of Paleozoic structures (De Grave et al. 2007 and others); absolute-dated total right-lateral slip across Altay 2.0-9.0 mm/yr; Calais et al. 2003 GPS first-order 4-7 mm/yr; "10-20% of the shortening induced by the India-Asia convergence is accommodated along the major faults of the Altay" (Calais 2003, 2006; Wang 2001); Har-Us-Nuur slip rates questioned. https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2025/egusphere-2025-5016/egusphere-2025-5016.pdf
- Nissen et al. 2009 EPSL (full text read): Har-Us-Nuur fault horizontal slip 2.4 +/- 0.4 mm/yr (10Be fan offsets); fault initiation ~2 Ma; "significantly later than the late Oligocene to Miocene (28-5 Ma) onset of shortening in the Altai region"; interpreted as deformation widening over time to new range-margin faults. Cumulative throw ~2000 m (range peneplain ~3500 m vs low bedrock 1500-1600 m). Paper gives India-Eurasia convergence "~35-40 mm/yr" (GPS-constrained). Altai onset "poorly constrained": Oligocene-Miocene basin coarsening; AFT Pliocene in Russian Altai and Baatar Hyarhan, Miocene in Chinese Altai. https://web.uvic.ca/~enissen/papers/nissen-etal-2009-EPSL.pdf
- Ramel 2025 also cites Vassallo 2006 Hovd and Ha 2023 ranges of morphotectonic slip 0.5-2.8 mm/yr. Ramel 2025 abstract: GPS dataset in 2010 by Calais 2006 "about two decades old". (from preprint, read).
- Mongolian Altai shortening (search summary; Höh Serh-Tsagaan Salaa fault): minimum shortening 0.3 +/- 0.1 mm/yr; minimum dextral slip 0.9 mm/yr; ~20% of Indo-Asian deformation in Mongolian Altai on discrete faults. Source: Search summary citing eprints.gla.ac.uk/8470 and peeref 9042887; UNVERIFIED primary.
- Tien Shan GPS (search summary, Zubovich 2010 Tectonics; primary not reachable: MIT DSpace returned bot-check/redirect): Tarim thrust beneath Tien Shan ~4-7 mm/yr; shortening across Chatkal ~4 mm/yr bound; Talas-Ferghana slip <2 mm/yr; northern Pamir edge >=10-15 mm/yr; 1-3 mm/yr absorbed farther north at Dzungarian Alatau. https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010Tecto..29.6014Z UNVERIFIED

## 12. Verified from full text: Tarim and Tian Shan foreland (Richter et al.)
- Tarim Sea retreat at ca. 34 Ma; "westward retreat of the Tarim Sea ... resulted from a combination of uplift and deformation related to the Indo-Asian collision and eustatic sea-level" (full text read). https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10402322
- Erg desert at ca. 12 Ma adjacent to southern Tian Shan in NW Tarim; "only after ca. 12 Ma did the Pamir-Tian Shan create a high-elevation barrier ... forming a rain shadow in the northwestern Tarim Basin" (full text). https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10402322
- Southern Tian Shan foreland total shortening 35.3 +/- 3.5 km (range 23-50 km in earlier work); deformation above fault T3 initiated ~12 Ma (thermochronology, Lu et al. 2021), T4 and T5 only after 6.0 Ma (between 6.0 and 3.5 Ma initiation); average shortening since 12 Ma ~3 mm/yr (full text). https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10402322
- Foreland-to-interior migration: foreland migrated southward after 7 Ma (full text). https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10402322
- Tarim lake record (PNAS 2014, HTML, abstract and text read): Tibetan uplift and Cenozoic global cooling both proposed for Asian interior aridification; "relative roles ... are often difficult to disentangle"; Lop Nor core site ~800 m asl in eastern basin; Tarim record covers last ~7 Myr. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4246285
- Implication for the tool: rain shadow and interior drying on the Tarim side followed range growth by ~10 Myr in this record (not instantaneous).

## 13. Anatolian plateau (Molin et al. 2023, Tectonics, full text read)
- Eastern Anatolian Plateau (EAP): continent-continent collisional plateau at early stage; surface uplift ~2000 m, of which ~500 m dynamically supported; mean elevation of the surface ~2000 m; endorheic basins; thinned lithospheric mantle (seismic). https://iris.uniroma3.it/retrieve/cdea49ae-acb9-4ab7-aee9-5095c7b02867/Molin-et-al-Tectonics_2023.pdf
- Uplift in two steps: first at 10-11 Ma (slab window and mantle flow from Arabia), second at ~5 Ma (continued inflow plus isostasy). Same source.
- Crustal thickening alone cannot explain EAP topography; dynamic support by mantle flow invoked (low-velocity anomaly, positive free-air gravity, positive residual topography, volcanism 11 Ma to present). Same source.
- Global context in the same paper: continental plateaus mean elevation >1000 m, 400 m above continental mean of 600 m (Babault and Van der Driessen 2013, cited therein). Same source. This gives a comparison point for the owner's 1000-2000 m target: a plateau of 1000-2000 m is typical of "plateau" definitions, not extreme.
- Molin's "rigid" caveat: the EAP is "at an early stage of development" (abstract).

## 15. Tian Shan onset lag and Bogda Shan (Jiao et al. 2021, Tectonophysics; full text read)
- Jiao et al. state: "many studies have documented a marked increase in the uplift or exhumation rates at ~25 Ma or later on the Junggar margin of Tian Shan, much later than the onset of the India-Asia collision"; open question whether the lag reflects northward propagation of deformation. https://gfzpublic.gfz-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_5008231_2/component/file_5008322/5008231.pdf
- Bogda Shan northern piedmont shortening: <1 mm/yr across foreland fold-thrust belt in the western Bogda (~10 km piedmont), vs 3-5 mm/yr west of Urumqi (Wu et al. 2016, cited therein). Same source.
- Jiao's own kinematic model predicts Bogda Shan Cenozoic uplift initiated before ~40 Ma (model result, "thrusting ... at 47-31 Ma"); this is their model, not an observation. Same source.
- Note: the Huangfu et al. 2021 GRL claim (Tian Shan lies ~1500 km north of the boundary; major Cenozoic uplift >30 Myr after collision onset) is still only from a search summary of the GRL abstract (gdlab.ucas.edu.cn and vufind mirror). The full text was not reachable. The summary states both a ">30 Myr" uplift lag and a ">30 Myr time lag between model results and constraints", which contradict each other. UNVERIFIED. Treat the 30 Myr figure as unresolved.
- Also: Jiao et al. (sec. "Tian Shan") treats mountain-range crust as rigid blocks bounded by thrust faults: rock uplift from hanging-wall throw proportional to slip rate for a given dip (method, same source).

## 16. Cratonic/rigid block Te (search summaries; mixed)
- Tarim: 66 +/- 7 km heat-flow Te (Burov 1995 method, previously noted); thin-plate flexure of Tarim and Qaidam: Te up to 110 km and 70 km (search summary; likely Braitenberg 2002, UNVERIFIED primary); both studies treat Tarim as rigid block.
- Ordos: named as high-Te rigid block (heat-flow study), no km value in excerpt. UNVERIFIED.
- Sichuan: coseismic-subsidence study best fit Te 30-40 km; Longmen Shan foreland study western Sichuan 10-20 km, middle 30-40 km. UNVERIFIED primaries.
- Siberian craton: central Eurasia study finds parts of Siberian craton, North China, Tarim and Turan with maximum lithospheric Te; platform-flexure: flexural rigidity ~5e23 N m for East Siberian and East European cratons, equivalent to Te ~30-40 km (search summary). UNVERIFIED.
- Caveat from the summary: these methods (heat flow, gravity, coseismic, platform flexure) give non-comparable numbers. Tarim range 66-110 km reflects method spread.
- Source for the summary set: https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2009/EGU2009-362.pdf (Sichuan, read earlier); https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/lithospheric-elastic-thickness-estimates-in-central-eurasia/ (central Eurasia Te, abstract only) UNVERIFIED.

## Key takeaways for the tool
1. Lag: far-field uplift lags collision onset by a long time, not a fixed speed. Evidence: Junggar-margin Tian Shan uplift/exhumation increase ~25 Ma (Jiao et al. 2021, full text); Altai onset 28-5 Ma with Har-Us-Nuur faulting only ~2 Ma (Nissen 2009, full text); Tarim Sea retreat ~34 Ma (Richter, full text); Qaidam basin fill ~25.5 Ma (search summary). Suggested rule (design choice, not a source number): onset_far = t_collision + lag, lag roughly 10-30 Myr at 1500-2500 km, with the lag shortened where old structures are present. Mark the lag range as UNVERIFIED in the tool.
2. Reactivation localizes on inherited structure: Transbaikal Mesozoic grabens reactivated hundreds of km away (Jolivet 2013, full text); Altai Paleozoic structures reactivated, 20-5 Ma (Ramel 2025, full text); Atlas Triassic-Liassic faults inverted (search summary). Rule: a map of old belts with flags for "weak" (Paleozoic/Mesozoic rift or suture) and "strong" (craton); activate the weak ones at distance first.
3. Rigid blocks take little internal shortening and push deformation to their margins: Tarim, Ordos, Siberia have high Te (Tarim 66 km heat-flow, up to 110 km flexure; Sichuan 10-40 km), Tarim interior elevations 780-1300 m (HK sheet, UNVERIFIED primary). Rule: stiff cells (Te high) get low relief and fault-bounded margins; weak cells (Te low, e.g. western Sichuan 10-20 km) absorb more strain.
4. Mongolian bowl: plateau mean ~915-1525 m (Wikipedia, UNVERIFIED primary) and Hangai dome interior >4000 m only locally. Mechanism unresolved: mantle upwelling (Zhejiang MTZ study, abstract), delamination (LAB seismic, abstract), or isostasy from crustal thickening; Jolivet 2013 says no wide plume is imaged; Durham page says heat flow is moderate. The tool should not force one mechanism; expose "dynamic support" as a parameter (Molin 2023 estimates ~500 m dynamic support of ~2000 m uplift in Eastern Anatolia as a comparable number).
5. Shortening budgets to scale far-field relief: Altai 10-20% of India-Asia shortening (Calais 2003/2006 via Ramel 2025, UNVERIFIED primary); Altai dextral 4-6 mm/yr (Ramel 2025 full text); Tian Shan foreland shortening 35.3 +/- 3.5 km, ~3 mm/yr since 12 Ma (Richter, full text); Bogda piedmont <1 mm/yr vs 3-5 mm/yr west of Urumqi (Jiao 2021, full text). Far-field rates are a few mm/yr, not tens; so the far-field relief budget is small per My, and accumulates over 10-30 Myr.
6. Rain shadow: interior drying follows range growth with a lag of about 10 Myr in the Tarim record (barrier formed after ~12 Ma, Richter full text). Tarim lake disappearance ~4.9 Ma (PNAS, from search summary). Interpretation disputed: global cooling vs tectonic uplift (PNAS: "difficult to disentangle"). Rule: climate can be a flag or a lagged function of range height, not a hard causal link.
7. Distance: the far-field reach is roughly 1500-2500 km from the front in these sources (Tian Shan ~1500 km; Altai, Baikal >2000 km; Tapponnier 1982 indenter penetration >=2000 km). The owner's "thousands of km" is at the upper bound and is not directly supported beyond ~2500 km by any read source. Use a decay with distance rather than a hard cutoff.
8. Caveats: Calais 2006 (key GPS source for Mongolia) could not be read; Huangfu 2021 "30 Myr lag" conflicts with its own summary text; several Qilian/Kunlun dates disagree by up to ~20 Myr (early Paleocene to late Miocene). Prefer the Jiao 2021, Nissen 2009, Richter et al. (GSA Bulletin), Molin 2023 full-text values in the tool.
