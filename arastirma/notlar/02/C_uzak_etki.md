# C. Far field deformation and rigid blocks (working notes, 2026-11-11)
Convention: every fact is followed by the URL it was read at. UNVERIFIED = not confirmed from a source I read in full. No em/en dashes.

## 1. Tien Shan: distance, lag, rates, reactivation
- Tian Shan lies ~1500 km north of the India-Asia plate boundary; major Cenozoic uplift came >30 Myr after collision onset (Huangfu et al. 2021 GRL, abstract level only, full text not read) https://gdlab.ucas.edu.cn/index.php/en/yjfx/2020-03-27-06-27-26/3077-2021-geophysical-research-letters-2 (via search summary; UNVERIFIED in detail)
- Huangfu et al. 2021 2D thermomechanical models: direct Indian lithospheric mantle vs rigid Tarim lithospheric mantle contact beneath western Tibet dominates Tian Shan building; models vary thermorheology and convergence rate. (search summary only) https://zenodo.org/record/4818316 (dataset), UNVERIFIED
- GPS: N-S shortening across Tianshan at ~76E "at least ~20 mm/yr"; South Tianshan 75-78E convergence 15-24 mm/yr; Pamir-South Tianshan junction takes ~68% of total convergence, ~58% in intermontane faults/basins in north of South Tianshan; deformation attenuates west to east and south to north with distance from Pamir. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2021.793890/full
- Other GPS numbers (from search summary, not verified at source): ~20 mm/yr west of Issyk Kul decreasing to ~9 mm/yr over ~500 km eastward; ~12 mm/yr across northern 2/3 of central Tien Shan; ~8 mm/yr eastern Tian Shan. (search summaries; need primary: Zubovich 2010, Reigber 2001) UNVERIFIED
- Reactivation: Zhao et al. 2025 (Solid Earth 16, 503), Harlik Mountains, easternmost Tianshan: relict low-relief surfaces (AFT ~110-100 Ma) formed after Mongol-Okhotsk collapse; fault zones cool 90-70 Ma; "by the Oligocene (~30 Ma), far-field effects from India-Eurasia collision reactivated major faults in a left-lateral sense, driving uplift, tilting, incision"; Cenozoic exhumation of Harlik <2 km; relict surfaces now at 3000-3500 m (in places); Cenozoic ranges uplifted "mainly along pre-existing faults, resulting in a basin-and-range-type landscape". https://se.copernicus.org/articles/16/503/2025/ and https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2024/egusphere-2024-3668/egusphere-2024-3668-manuscript-version3.pdf
- Takeaway from same paper: Mesozoic low-relief surfaces survive inside active ranges because erosion rates are low; far-field belts inherit old planation surfaces. same URL.

## 2. Pamir indenter and Tarim as rigid block
- Pamir protrudes ~300 km north relative to Tibet, between Tajik basin and cratonic Tarim; Indian mantle indenter underthrusts Tarim along a compressive transform boundary (Bloch et al. 2021 GRL). https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/531322/1/2021GL095413.pdf

## 3. Effective elastic thickness (Te) of rigid blocks
- Te of Tarim basin 66+-7 km from heat flow (Burov 1995 method), larger than crustal thickness 45 km; low-heat-flow blocks Tarim, Junggar, Ordos, Sichuan have mantle dominated strength; seismicity and active faults localize at boundaries of high Te blocks (Tarim, Ordos); Wenchuan EQ at contrast Sichuan block vs weak Tibet margin (Liu and Wang, EGU2009-362 abstract). https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2009/EGU2009-362.pdf
- Other values (search summary only, UNVERIFIED): Braitenberg 2002 Te up to 110 km Tarim, 70 km Qaidam; Tien Shan flexure study Te 40-50 km in Tarim/Junggar/Kazakh to 20-23 km beneath Tien Shan.

## 4. Mongolian plateau (search-level, to be verified)
- Hangay Dome ~200,000 km2 within Mongolian Plateau ~425,000 km2; dome interior locally >4000 m with high-elevation low-relief paleo-erosion surface; basalts 30 Ma to present; uplift recent, cause debated. https://eesarchive.lehigh.edu/groups/mongolia/indepth/hightopography.html
