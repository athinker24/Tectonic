## 1. Indenter and escape experiments

- Tapponnier et al. 1982 (Geology, "Propagating extrusion tectonics in Asia"): PDF fetched but is a scanned image with no text layer; NOT read directly. Secondary summary only (web search snippet): India penetration rotated Indochina ~25 deg and extruded ~800 km SE. UNVERIFIED from primary. https://earthjay.com/earthquakes/20210521_china/tapponnier_etal_1982_extrusion_tectonics_asia.pdf
- Davy and Cobbold 1988 (Bull. Geol. Inst. Uppsala 14, 129-141), gravity-scaled multilayer experiments, READ: https://www.paleoarchive.com/literature/Davy&Cobbold1988-IndentationTectonicsNatureExperiment1.pdf
  - Outcome depends mainly on lateral confinement of the indented continent; also layer rheology and indenter/continent dimensions. https://www.paleoarchive.com/literature/Davy&Cobbold1988-IndentationTectonicsNatureExperiment1.pdf
  - Strong confinement: crustal thickening only, localized in a plateau in front of the indenter, by serial propagation of arcuate thrusts away from the indenter; slip vectors radial; arc parallel extension in forethrust hangingwalls, arc parallel contraction in backthrust hangingwalls; vertical axis block rotations. https://www.paleoarchive.com/literature/Davy&Cobbold1988-IndentationTectonicsNatureExperiment1.pdf
  - Weak confinement on one side: escape toward the free margin, one main strike slip (wrench) zone from the indenter corner to the free margin; mostly wrenches and rifts. Moderate confinement: thickening and escape in roughly equal proportion; plateau in front of indenter, wrench zone starting at the plateau corner. https://www.paleoarchive.com/literature/Davy&Cobbold1988-IndentationTectonicsNatureExperiment1.pdf
  - Narrower indenter (half width): narrower but longer (in the shortening direction) plateau with more thrusts, more curved arcuate thrusts, escaping triangle rotates clockwise up to ~20 deg. https://www.paleoarchive.com/literature/Davy&Cobbold1988-IndentationTectonicsNatureExperiment1.pdf
  - Always: plateau of thickened crust in front of indenter, one main wrench zone from the indenter corner, escaping triangle. https://www.paleoarchive.com/literature/Davy&Cobbold1988-IndentationTectonicsNatureExperiment1.pdf
  - They did NOT see serial escape of successive blocks (as in Tapponnier 1982 plane strain models); they argue plane strain models differ from theirs. DISAGREEMENT between analogue designs. https://www.paleoarchive.com/literature/Davy&Cobbold1988-IndentationTectonicsNatureExperiment1.pdf
  - Simple elastic half space line load solution (stress trajectories confocal ellipses/hyperbolae) explains initiation of arcuate thrusts in front of a narrow indenter (their own interpretation). https://www.paleoarchive.com/literature/Davy&Cobbold1988-IndentationTectonicsNatureExperiment1.pdf
  - Ratio R (indenting stress times indenter width/continent width over confinement stress): high R (weak confinement or wide indenter) means more lateral escape. https://www.paleoarchive.com/literature/Davy&Cobbold1988-IndentationTectonicsNatureExperiment1.pdf
  - A small continent can indent a large one if laterally supported. https://www.paleoarchive.com/literature/Davy&Cobbold1988-IndentationTectonicsNatureExperiment1.pdf

## 2. Himalayan arc, syntaxes (Bajolet et al. 2013, Tectonics 32:1529, READ)
- Himalaya is convex toward the overriding plate (unusual; Andes, Alps, Bitlis concave, Pyrenees straight); two large scale syntaxes bounded by Sagaing (E) and Chaman (W) N-S strike slip faults; two small scale sharp curves at Nanga Parbat (W) and Namche Barwa (E). https://www.isterre.fr/IMG/pdf/bajolet_2013_curvaturehimalaya.pdf
- Silicone/glucose experiments: convex range with syntaxes for weak subduction interface; concave without syntaxes for strong interface; convex curvature forms because upper plate overthrusts faster at center than at the ends, with flat slab; lateral decoupling at indenter sides gives sharper syntaxes; high buoyancy number (thick weak upper plate) gives collapse, more lateral escape and larger curvature. https://www.isterre.fr/IMG/pdf/bajolet_2013_curvaturehimalaya.pdf
- Authors scale experiment curvature to 310 to 400 km after 45 My for two experiments (their extrapolation). https://www.isterre.fr/IMG/pdf/bajolet_2013_curvaturehimalaya.pdf

- Bajolet: Himalayan convexity measured the same way as their models is ~350 km; experiments reproduce ~15 My, extrapolated linearly to 310 to 400 km at 45 My. https://www.isterre.fr/IMG/pdf/bajolet_2013_curvaturehimalaya.pdf

## 3. Adria as indenter (Le Breton et al. 2017, Tectonics 36, READ)
- Best fit: Adria moved 113 km to NW (azimuth 325) and rotated 5 +/- 3 deg CCW relative to Europe since 20 Ma, about a pole in Spain (38.2N, 3.16W); CCW rotation estimates in literature range from a few degrees to ~20 deg. https://www.geo.fu-berlin.de/en/geol/fachrichtungen/geologie/mitarbeiter/Professoren/elinelebreton/LeBreton_etal_17.pdf
- Post-20 Ma convergence: 110 km vs Moesia, 125 km in Eastern Alps (range 115 to 150), 30 to 113 km western Alps, 60 km extension in Sicily Channel. https://www.geo.fu-berlin.de/en/geol/fachrichtungen/geologie/mitarbeiter/Professoren/elinelebreton/LeBreton_etal_17.pdf
- Indentation of Alps began ~30 Ma (west, crustal wedging) and 23 to 21 Ma (east); amount of Neogene indentation varies along strike and is related to Adria rotation. https://www.geo.fu-berlin.de/en/geol/fachrichtungen/geologie/mitarbeiter/Professoren/elinelebreton/LeBreton_etal_17.pdf
- Western Alps wedge (Ivrea body) acted as a pivot and stopped further NW motion; Africa push + Adriatic-Hellenide slab pull to NE. Plate convergence exceeds measured Apennine/Dinaride shortening, attributed to crust-mantle decoupling during rollback (Apennines). https://www.geo.fu-berlin.de/en/geol/fachrichtungen/geologie/mitarbeiter/Professoren/elinelebreton/LeBreton_etal_17.pdf
- Implication for the tool: the indenter pivots at one corner so shortening increases away from the pivot (Eastern Alps 115 to 150 km vs W Alps 30 to 113 km), giving a wedge shaped, along strike varying belt. (my inference from these numbers)

## 4. Alpine vs Apennine style (Carminati, Doglioni, Scrocca 2004, IGC32 volume, READ; Doglioni school view, a conference paper, not a full review)
Source for all lines in this section: https://elearning.uniroma1.it/mod/resource/view.php?id=180442 (PDF text)
- Alps: convergence faster than slab retreat, double vergent, high morphological and structural elevation, deep rocks involved (UHP; core eroded several tens of km), basal decollement involves crust and lithospheric mantle of both plates, thickened lithosphere (150 to 200 km cited), no back-arc basin, highest peak Mont Blanc 4810 m.
- Apennines: slab retreat faster than convergence, single vergent, low elevation, only a few km of overburden removed, mainly sedimentary rocks, only shallow crust of the lower plate involved, back-arc (Tyrrhenian) basin, new shallow Tyrrhenian Moho (~20 km) over deeper Adriatic Moho (~52 km) at Val Tiberina; highest Gran Sasso 2914 m.
- They cite Waschbusch and Beaumont 1996 numerical models: slab retreat slower than convergence gives double vergent belt (Alps); faster gives single vergent belt plus back-arc basin (Apennines). Same idea cited as Royden and Burchfiel 1989, Royden 1993.
- Elevation of Apennines is higher where the basal decollement is deeper (about 10 km in W Adriatic vs about 3 km in Ionian); Apennine tectonic wave migrates 10 to 20 mm/yr (their figure) vs erosion <1 mm/yr, so peaks sit east of the water divide.
- Foreland monocline dip: Alps 2 to 5 deg, Apennines 4 to 10 deg; Apennine foredeep subsidence >1 mm/yr vs Alps <0.3 mm/yr.
- Caveat: Doglioni favours westward lithosphere drift relative to mantle as the cause of asymmetry; other authors use slab pull; this is a debate (they say so themselves in the text).

## 5. Rollback rates, Apennine segmentation (Rosenbaum and Piana Agostinetti 2015, Tectonics 34, READ)
Source: https://boa.unimib.it/retrieve/e39773b8-5dbe-35a3-e053-3a05fe0aac26/37_rosenbaum2015_LSL_review.pdf
- Calabrian slab segment is narrow (~500 km) and rolled back ultrafast (60 to 100 km/Ma); northern Apennine rollback inferred at 10 to 15 km/Ma on two sides of the Livorno-Sillaro lineament (15.2 and 10.6 km/Ma). Crust thins from ~50 km under the Apennines to ~20 km at Tyrrhenian coast. Along strike segmentation of rollback rate (tear) produces a left bend/orogenic curvature.
- Slab width controls rollback (cites Schellart et al. 2007): narrow slabs retreat faster. (cited claim in the paper, not tested by me)

## 6. Pamir and Hindu Kush salient (Ge et al. 2022, Front. Earth Sci. 10:967529, review, READ)
Source for this section: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2022.967529/pdf
- Pamir is a northward convex salient made of arcuate structures extending hundreds of km, at the western tip of the Himalaya-Tibet orogen.
- TWO END MEMBER MODELS (disagreement): (a) arc formed by ~300 km northward overthrusting of the Pamir over its foreland (Burtman and Molnar 1993; Cowgill 2010; Sobel 2013); (b) arc mostly inherited from pre-Cenozoic structure with <110 km northward movement in the Cenozoic (Chen et al. 2018; Li et al. 2020).
- Seismic imaging studies read the cratonic Indian lithosphere as an indenter forcing delamination and rollback of the Pamir/Asian lithosphere into an arcuate Pamir slab (Kufner 2016; Bloch 2021); slab tear/overturn under E Pamir linked to along strike variability of indenter tip. Collision of Indian craton with Pamir slab may have started ~12 to 11 Ma using 34 mm/yr and 380 to 400 km delamination (a derived estimate in that review).
- Strike slip bounding faults: Darvaz (sinistral, west side), Kashgar-Yecheng transfer system (dextral, east side); Darvaz GPS rates 8 +/- 1 mm/yr (N-S) and ~15 mm/yr (NEE segment); Burtman and Molnar inferred 10 to 15 mm/yr. Tajik fold and thrust belt in the west is westward convex with E-W shortening of ~70 to ~240 km (range between models). KSES (Kongur Shan extensional system) ~250 km long.
- Hybrid model "northward indentation and westward extrusion": E Pamir moves en bloc N, W Pamir collapses and extrudes west (Schurr 2014; Metzger 2020).
- Rotation sense: radial thrusting model gives CCW rotation on the west and CW on the east side of the salient.
Secondary only (web search snippet, not opened): analogue modelling treating Hindu Kush and Pamir as two indenters on the Tajik basin gives a ~300 km wide fold-thrust belt with ~90 deg curvature. UNVERIFIED. Pamir shortening ~13 to 15 mm/yr vs total India-Asia ~34 mm/yr at that longitude (snippet from Ge/Metzger type papers; the 34 mm/yr value is in Ge et al. above).

## 7. Progressive arcs, rigid vs deformable indenters (Jimenez-Bonilla et al. 2020, Front. Earth Sci. 8:72, READ)
Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2020.00072/pdf
- Previous analogue models of curved fold-and-thrust belts used a RIGID indenter of fixed shape and size; the authors say these "failed to reproduce some conspicuous features" of arcs (vertical-axis rotations, divergent transport around the indenter). Source: same PDF, section before "THE GIBRALTAR ARC EXTERNAL WEDGE".
- Their fix: a deformable, flexible backstop that deforms in map view and whose curvature increases, so arc amplitude grows as radius of curvature decreases ("progressive arc"). Source: same PDF, abstract.
- Gibraltar external wedge chord ~185 to 290 km, protrusion degree 0.4 to 0.5, compared with Calabrian and Carpathian arcs (similar values). Source: same PDF, conclusions.
- Calabrian arc: blocks ~200 km long rotated clockwise and counterclockwise in NE and SW limbs (Cifelli et al. 2008, 2016, as cited); Southern Apennines Miocene rotations up to ~100 deg, ~56 deg in the last 9 Ma (cited). Source: same PDF, conclusions.
- Caveat: the authors say the Southern Apennines and Sicilian Maghrebides did not detach over evaporites, unlike the Gibraltar arc, so model comparison is partial. Source: same PDF, conclusions.

## 14. NW Himalayan syntaxis, oblique edges sandbox (Replumaz et al. 2012, Aust. J. Earth Sci., READ)
Source: https://www.isterre.fr/IMG/pdf/Replumaz_2012_NWhimalayanSyntaxis.pdf
- Two sandbox edges oblique to convergence (Chaman fault west, Karakorum fault east); brittle layer ~30 km thick. Source: same PDF, abstract and set-up.
- Obliquity generated E-W shortening up to 40% of N-S convergence; strike slip on border faults up to 60% of convergence vector. Source: same PDF, abstract.
- Oblique edges alone did not produce a N-S axial fold like Nanga Parbat in their set up; with a free southern boundary, pure strike slip faults plus N-S fold appeared. Source: same PDF, abstract.
- Nanga Parbat: crustal scale N-S elongated dome; rapid exhumation since 5 Ma, extension since 2 Ma (Schneider et al. 1999, as cited). Source: same PDF, geology section.
- Implication for the tool: a syntaxis can be modeled as a corner where edges of an indenter (or of the wedge) are oblique, and this obliquity itself generates strike slip and perpendicular folds. Single paper analogue claim.

## Key takeaways for the tool
1. Indenter shape alone does not set belt shape. Confinement and indenter width decide whether the belt thickens (plateau with arcuate thrusts) or escapes (wrench zone from indenter corner). Davy and Cobbold 1988, https://www.paleoarchive.com/literature/Davy&Cobbold1988-IndentationTectonicsNatureExperiment1.pdf (analogue, single lab series). Tool rule: if one side is free, put a strike slip wrench from the indenter corner to that edge; if both sides are confined, use arcuate thrusts in front of the indenter.
2. Rigid indenters did not produce progressive arcs with vertical axis rotation; a map-view deformable backstop with growing curvature did. Jimenez-Bonilla et al. 2020, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2020.00072/pdf (analogue). Tool rule: model curvature growth over time by increasing the arc's curvature each step, not by a fixed indenter.
3. Oblique edges of an indenter or wedge generate lateral (strike slip) components and orthogonal folds; up to 40% E-W shortening from N-S convergence in one sandbox. Replumaz et al. 2012, https://www.isterre.fr/IMG/pdf/Replumaz_2012_NWhimalayanSyntaxis.pdf. Tool rule: syntaxes come from oblique boundary edges.
4. Indenter corners are where strike slip faults bound the indenter and where stress rotates 10 to 20 deg over ~70 km. Reiter et al. 2018, https://opac.geologie.ac.at/ais312/dokumente/Reiter_et_al-2018-Tectonics.pdf (Adria, Dolomites indenter).
5. Slab rollback sets Apennine vs Alpine style: faster rollback than convergence gives single vergent, low, back-arc basin; slower gives double vergent, high. Carminati, Doglioni and Scrocca 2004, https://elearning.uniroma1.it/mod/resource/view.php?id=180442 (Doglioni school, conference). Also Rosenbaum and Piana Agostinetti 2015, https://boa.unimib.it/retrieve/e39773b8-5dbe-35a3-e053-3a05fe0aac26/37_rosenbaum2015_LSL_review.pdf: narrow slabs roll back faster (cited claim). Tool rule: two knobs, convergence rate vs rollback rate, and slab width.
6. Elevation is contested. Dielforder et al. 2020 claim height is set by megathrust shear force, not climate, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-020-2340-7 (abstract only, single paper claim). Peltzer 1988 gives thickening per indenter width, https://www.paleoarchive.com/literature/Peltzer1988-CentrifugedExperimentsContinentalScaleTectonicsAsia.pdf. Tool rule: the Apennine vs Alps height difference can be a parameter (basal decollement depth, 3 to 10 km per Carminati et al.) rather than a derived quantity.
7. Microcontinents: no source read that explains why a block is tall. Taiwan shows prism crest 2000 to 3000 m above modeled values north of 22 N after a second collision stage (Tan et al. 2022, https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feart.2022.941040/full). Southern Alps uplift up to 1 cm/yr with ~27 mm/yr shortening (McGrath et al. 2025). Treat "tall microcontinent" as a designer choice: a thick crust plus high convergence rate in its region.
8. Disagreements to flag in the tool docs: Taiwan collision is southward propagating (Byrne) vs two-stage simultaneous (Tan 2022); Pamir arc is 300 km overthrust vs inherited (Ge et al. 2022, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2022.967529/pdf); Iran escape is denied by Allen and Blanc 2006 (snippet).
9. Number checks for the tool: Himalayan convexity ~350 km (Bajolet 2013); Gibraltar chord 185 to 290 km; Calabrian blocks ~200 km; Alpine shortening 115 to 150 km east and 30 to 113 km west. Total along strike lengths of Himalaya, Alps, Apennines: NOT found in this run.
10. Single paper claims (do not rely on alone): Dielforder 2020 height law; Replumaz 2012 oblique edge sandbox; Peltzer 1988 centrifuge; Reiter 2018 stress and indenter boundaries; Jimenez-Bonilla 2020 deformable backstop.
