## HIMALAYA (central Nepal / Ganges foreland)
Source: DeCelles & Carrapa 2021 JGR Solid Earth 126, e2020JB021256 (full text read) https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10229963
- Indian basement flexed ~5-6.5 km below sea level directly adjacent to Main Frontal thrust (MFT). https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10229963
- Foredeep fill (Miocene to modern) thickens from 1000 to ~5000 m over ~190 km south-to-north. https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10229963
- Flexural rigidity used for foreland plate: 3.0e23 to 1.0e24 N m, loaded by half-loads giving average topography 150-200 km wide and 4 km high. https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10229963
- Present taper, central Nepal (own 16 DEM profiles, ~16.5 km spacing, ~150 km long, N18.5E): surface slope alpha ~0.1-0.55 deg in foredeep/Chitwan wedge-top basin; Lesser Himalaya average ~1.6 deg; Greater Himalaya average ~7.4-7.6 deg (range 5.3-15.8 deg). Steep Greater Himalayan south face is only 20-40 km wide. https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10229963
- Basal decollement dip beta: ~3 deg under proximal foreland basin, ~3.5 deg under Chitwan basin, ~6.6 deg under Lesser Himalaya, ~9.1 deg north of Main Central Thrust (>10 deg at rear). Decollement depth ~6.5 km at 30 km north of the front, ~11 km at 60 km; >18 km north of ~80 km. https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10229963
- Total taper (alpha+beta): undeformed foredeep ~2.6-3.1 deg; Chitwan + Lesser Himalaya ~4-8 deg; Lesser Himalaya 6.5-8.5 deg. Greater Himalaya alpha>7 deg and beta>9 deg is incompatible with a purely brittle critical wedge (brittle-ductile transition, citing Williams 1994). https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10229963
- MFT propagated ~85 km southward in early Miocene into foredeep, followed by ~42 km shortening of those rocks; Main Boundary thrust slip ~100 km order. https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10229963

## ZAGROS (Arabia-Eurasia)
Source: Mouthereau 2011 Geol. Mag. 148, 726-738 "Timing of uplift in the Zagros belt/Iranian plateau and accommodation of late Cenozoic Arabia-Eurasia convergence" (full text read) https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/19E9DEA9D3C59A77EFB14BFA4B36A1E2/S0016756811000306a.pdf/timing_of_uplift_in_the_zagros_beltiranian_plateau_and_accommodation_of_late_cenozoic_arabiaeurasia_convergence.pdf
- Total convergence since ~22 Ma (stable northward drift) predicted 440 km; velocity quoted ~19-26 mm/yr overall Arabia-Eurasia, current shortening in Zagros 7-10 mm/yr. Same URL.
- Crust 45 km thick under Zagros Folded Belt and High Zagros (unthickened Arabian margin); Moho 70 km under Sanandaj-Sirjan belt; Iranian block north of SSZ crust ~50 km, warm mantle lithosphere to ~100 km. Same URL.
- Mean Iranian plateau elevation ~1500 m (Hatzfeld & Molnar 2010, as cited); Central Iran crust ~42 km (48 km near Alborz); Zagros/plateau southern edge marked by cumulative topographic step ~2 km. Same URL.
- Basement thrust units spaced ~80 km apart in Fars; topography interpreted as crustal-scale critically tapering wedge; High Zagros is elevated low-relief area morphologically indistinguishable from the plateau edge. Same URL.
- Shortening budget in Fars section: 135 km total = 15 km (5%) in Zagros Folded Belt (post 12.4 Ma), ~50 km duplexing, ~70 km underthrusting below suture (post 25 Ma). 12 km thick sedimentary cover detachment-folded in ZFB (concentric long-wavelength folds). Iranian plateau 29% (38 km) shortening in Central Iran since 10 Ma; plateau 300-450 km wide giving 120-180 km shortening to build current crust. Same URL.
- Te of Arabian plate 50 km (Snyder & Barazangi 1986, as cited); topographic load wavelength ~100 km too small for local Airy. Same URL.
- Exhumation: local High Zagros rates 0.3-0.4 km/Myr; minimum 0.2 km/Myr; sediment accumulation rates in foreland 0.2-0.6 km/Myr. Same URL.

## PYRENEES (west-central transect, Jaca/Ebro foreland)
Source: Teixell, Labaume et al. 2016 Comptes Rendus Geoscience (CRGeos), doi 10.1016/j.crte.2015.10.010 (full text read) https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/geoscience/articles/10.1016/j.crte.2015.10.010/
- Ebro and Jaca are Cenozoic foreland basins separated by the External Sierras thrust front; External Sierras and Jaca basin detached in Upper Triassic Keuper evaporites/shales; synorogenic Tertiary succession "several thousand metres thick" (flysch and molasse). Same URL.
- Thick-skinned basement thrusts (Guarga, Gavarnie) uplift basin floor with structural relief ~9.5 km relative to regional Ebro basin basement elevation. Same URL.
- Lakora thrust displacement ~17 km; Larra ~5 km. Same URL.
- Total orogenic shortening of this transect ~114 km (present vs restored section, including exhumed mantle domain min. width); ~120 km for Pyrenees-Aquitaine system. Shortening split: ~15 km early (peridotite subduction), ~54 km protocollision, ~45 km full collision. Ansó-Arzacq section of Teixell 1998 is ~35 km less (~80 km). Same URL.
- Shortening rate: <1 mm/yr Late Cretaceous-Paleocene; ~3 mm/yr Early-Mid Eocene; ~2.5 mm/yr Late Eocene-Early Miocene (authors' own estimate, not a measurement). Same URL.
- Collision onset: protocollision probably Early Eocene (~54-50 Ma); full collision Late Eocene (cooling ~50 Ma; Gavarnie thrust Late Eocene-Early Oligocene; Guarga thrust Late Oligocene-Early Miocene = External Sierras emergence). Same URL.
- Restored pre-orogenic margins < 40 km wide (peridotite domain minimum 15 km). Same URL.
- Lower crustal Iberian slab reaches ~60 km depth (receiver functions, Chevrot et al. 2015, as cited). Same URL.

Other shortening estimates across Pyrenees (from search snippets, virtualexplorer.com.au, NOT yet verified from full text): ECORS profile 120-165 km (Roure 1989: 120; Muñoz 1992: 147; Fitzgerald 1999: 165); eastern section 125 km (Verges 1995); western central 48 km (Teixell 1996). Sources: https://virtualexplorer.com.au/article/2002/55/alpine-deformation-in-southern-pyrenees/intro.html and https://virtualexplorer.com.au/article/2002/58/the-pyrenean-orogen/tert.html. UNVERIFIED beyond snippet. Ebro basin width NOT found yet.
- Disagreement: plate kinematics require 160-180 km Iberia-Europe convergence since Late Cretaceous vs ~114-120 km geological shortening; Vissers & Meijer 2012, Mouthereau 2014 invoke hidden shortening via subduction. Same CRGeos URL (discussion section).

## TAIWAN (arc-continent collision, near steady state)
Source: Simoes, Avouac, Beyssac, Goffe, Farley & Chen 2007, JGR 112, B11405, doi 10.1029/2006JB004824 (full PDF read via pdftotext) https://basin.earth.ncu.edu.tw/download/lab%20reading/Taiwan%20geology/Simoes,%20S.etal._2007_Mountain%20building%20in%20Taiwan%20-%20A%20thermokinematic%20model.pdf
- Convergence ~90 mm/yr (GPS); ~42 mm/yr absorbed across the mountain belt; ~48 mm/yr on Philippine Sea side. Same URL.
- Model: shortening taken up on frontal foothill faults; crust thickened and exhumed by underplating beneath Tananao Complex (uplift ~6.3 mm/yr) and Hsueshan Range (~4.2 mm/yr). Frontal accretion <10% of incoming flux now, but ~80% before 4 Ma. Same URL.
- Steady state: Tananao units steady in cooling ages but not peak metamorphic T; Hsueshan units not yet exhumational steady state. Same URL.
- Undeformed Eurasian margin Moho at 30 km, dipping 11.5 deg east beneath the orogen (geophysics, as cited). Same URL.
- Wedge width ~96-98 km over northern Taiwan, "constant width", taken as steady state; erosion balances flux; wedge-scale erosion ~3.1 mm/yr average (pre-1.5 Ma), and erosion ~ underplating rate (topography steady). Same URL.
- Decollement beneath orogen needed to be ~17 deg steeper for northern-belt steady state (Simoes & Avouac 2006, as cited). Same URL.
- Propagation rate of collision southward 31 (+10/-5) mm/yr (Simoes & Avouac 2006) vs 55-90 mm/yr elsewhere cited. Same URL.
- Mass-balance: frontal material flux corresponds to top 7 km of Chinese upper crust underthrust; 76% of underthrust material subducted. Same URL.
- Critical-taper origin: Davis et al. 1983 (topographic slope uniform in western Taiwan), as cited by this paper; authors state it "calls for a significant contribution of underplating" beyond critical wedge (critical wedge growth by frontal accretion). Same URL.
- Search snippets (UNVERIFIED, abstract-level only): Stolar, Willett & Montgomery EPSL time to topographic steady state 1.8-2.3 Myr after emergence; rock residence 2-3 Myr; steady-state width ~90 km (conflicts with 96-98 km above). https://repository.geologyscience.ru/bitstreams/3f9b7a1a-1e23-4693-845c-68164c0303e7/download

## ALPS (Molasse foreland, slab rollback)
Source: Schlunegger & Kissling 2015, Nature Communications 6:8605, doi 10.1038/ncomms9605 (full text read) https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms9605 (PDF local copy read; URL from search: https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v6y2015i1d10.1038_ncomms9605.html)
- Balanced restoration gives ca. 160 km of crustal shortening in the Alps (citing Schmid et al. 1996, as cited). Same URL.
- Proximal orogen front moved 90-110 km north between 35 Ma and present; front propagation ~48 mm/yr before 30 Ma, <2 mm/yr 30-10 Ma, <0.5 mm/yr after. Same URL.
- Authors argue that flexure from topographic loads alone would require Alpine topography to rise since 30 Ma, inconsistent with paleoaltimetry (no rise since ~20 Ma); they propose slab rollback/load keeps Alpine surface low and constant. Same URL.
- Dispersal-system stream lengths W grew from ~60 km to ~100-140 km between 31 and 25 Ma, to ~150 km thereafter. Same URL.
- Te of the European plate: NOT found in this paper's snippet read; search results say Te values for Molasse show a very wide range (unverified, snippet only): https://www.geo.vu.nl/~andb/iberia/FlexureGeolsoc.pdf (0 chars, failed). 30 km Te claim: UNVERIFIED.
- Ziegler et al. 1996 (Central Alps, OCR of PDF very poor, numbers unreliable): convergence 500-550 km total (line 20 of OCR), Molasse basin "some 8500 km" of seismic lines (not a geometric width); numbers not usable without better copy. https://tecto.earth.unibas.ch/Members/Schmid/Publications/055_Ziegleretal1996.pdf
- Erosion (search snippet, UNVERIFIED): Wittmann et al. 0.9 +/- 0.3 mm/yr millennial erosion high crystalline Central Alps (10Be); Glotzbach et al. 2010 steady long-term exhumation Gotthard/Aar. ## MOUNTAIN MECHANICS (critical wedge, steady state, scaling)
Source: Willett, Slingerland & Hovius 2001, American Journal of Science 301, 455-485 (full PDF read) https://ajsonline.org/article/88260.pdf
- Critical wedge theory (Davis, Suppe & Dahlen 1983) predicts constant critical surface slope maintained by accretion; back-to-back wedges with topographic crest between. Same URL.
- Example: 100 km wide orogen uplifting at 1 mm/yr with velocity ratio R=20 corresponds to frontal accretion of a 5 km section at 20 mm/yr convergence. Same URL.
- Time to steady state scales with uplift rate, width and erosion parameters. For uplift rates 0.1-1 mm/yr and erosion numbers ~1, time to steady state ~1 to 50 Myr, longer at lower uplift; applies to orogens 50-200 km wide (Fig. 4 text). Same URL.
- Topographic steady state is NOT same as uplift = erosion pointwise; steady state requires erosion balance of full velocity field. Same URL.
- Search snippet (UNVERIFIED): Dahlen 1990 Annual Review gives tan(a+b) ~ a+b for narrow tapers (a surface slope, b basal dip, radians). Davis et al. 1983 JGR 88, B2, 1153-1172.
