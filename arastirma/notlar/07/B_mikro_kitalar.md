## 2. Crete (Hellenic arc)

- Area: ~8,450 km2 [Ö, Wikipedia summary]; 8,303 km2 and 8,335.88 km2 in other versions. CONFLICT.
- Highest point: Psiloritis (Mount Ida), 2,456 m. Consistent across sources [Ö].
- Uplift rate: 0.5–1.1 mm/yr along western and southern coasts; northern and eastern coasts roughly stable (EGU2018 abstract, https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2018/EGU2018-8852.pdf) [Ö]. Time-averaged 0–7 mm/yr (EGU2015 abstract, https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2015/EGU2015-2477.pdf) [Ö]. CONFLICT (method and period differ).
- Crust / Moho (receiver-function study, GJI 2003; https://www.dbpia.nl.go.kr/gji/article/155/2/733/599973) [Ö]:
  - Continental Moho ~31–34 km beneath S and E Crete; ~32 km W; ~39 km N.
  - Moho beneath Gavdos (south of Crete): 26 km.
  - Subducted oceanic Moho of African plate: 44–69 km beneath Gavdos and Crete.
- Gravity model: oceanic Moho ~50 km beneath Crete; thickened lower crust under upper continental crust [Ö].
- Surface-wave study: forearc crust ~43 km on average; northern Aegean ~28 km [Ö].
- CONFLICT: Continental Moho 31–39 km vs forearc average 43 km; oceanic Moho 40–69 km depending on method.
- Mean elevation, length/width: BULUNAMADI.

## 3. Cyprus

- Area: ~9,251 km2 (9,241 km2 land) [Ö]. Third-largest Mediterranean island after Sicily and Sardinia [Ö].
- Olympus: 1,952 m (most); 1,951 m and 1,953 m (others) [Ö].
- Troodos area: ~3,200 km2 vs ~3,500 km2 (~38% of island). CONFLICT [Ö].
- Crust: Troodos ophiolite (Upper Cretaceous oceanic crust/mantle). Island described as mix of oceanic and continental crustal fragments [Ö].
- Microcontinent link: Eratosthenes Seamount is an Early Cretaceous carbonate platform on a rifted continental fragment, with Miocene emergence and Pleistocene resubmergence (Government of Cyprus geology presentation, https://etgs.us/meeting_files/Geology%20of%20Cyprus-Final.pdf) [Ö]. Term "microcontinent" not used in the source. Closest evidence found.

## 5. Balearic Islands

- Balearic islands are emerged parts of the "Balearic Promontory", a continental block between Valencia Trough and Algerian Basin [Ö, ub.edu/geologica-acta search summaries].
- Rift timing:
  - Valencia Trough: Paleogene to early Miocene; upper Oligocene to early Miocene (two-phase model; first phase coeval with compression, second in middle Miocene) [Ö].
  - Three surrounding basins: 30–16 Ma (majority view) [Ö].
  - One study: 34–13 Ma [Ö].
  - Mesozoic extension: Jurassic sediments over thin crust in Valencia Trough, timing debated [Ö].
  - CONFLICT: 30–16 Ma vs 34–13 Ma vs Mesozoic onset.
- Mallorca: two Cenozoic rifting phases (Oligocene–early Miocene; later Serravallian ~14–11 Ma) [Ö].
- Puig Major 1,445 m: BULUNAMADI. Area and length: BULUNAMADI.

## 6. Alboran block

- Block status disputed: Copernicus preprint se-2019-122 (https://se.copernicus.org/preprints/se-2019-122/se-2019-122-AR2.pdf) says a rigid Alboran tectonic block cannot be defined structurally [Ö]. IGN describes Alboran as a microplate that migrated westward and collided with Iberia (https://www.ign.es/web/en/sismotectonica-por-zonas/alboran) [Ö]. CONFLICT.
- Crust: IGN 12–20 km (thinned continental crust) [Ö]; seismic: ~15 km in east Alborán basin, grading to oceanic-type crust farther east [Ö]; Rif crustal root >50 km (2014 AGU abstract; conference abstract, not peer-reviewed) [Ö].
- Origin models: slab rollback/delamination; Ligurian oceanic remnants (upper crust bodies at 5–10 km, velocity 6.5 km/s, tomography); stretched continental fragment from Balearic margin. Unresolved [Ö].

### 7.2 Seychelles

### 7.3 Zealandia
- Area: ~4.9 million km2; ~94% submerged [Ö, Wikipedia mirror]; one mirror gives 93%. CONFLICT (minor).
- Breakup from Gondwana: 79 Ma (Geology paper on IODP Exp. 371) [Ö]; 83–79 Ma (Wikipedia) [Ö]; 60–100 Ma (Live Science) [Ö]; 60–85 Ma from Australia and 85–130 Ma from Antarctica (mirror) [Ö]. CONFLICT.
- Tasman Sea: 73–68 Ma spreading in South Tasman; Coral Sea opening 62–52 Ma [Ö, Frontiers 2022].
- Submergence: possibly fully submerged by ~23 Ma [Ö].
- Lord Howe Rise ~2000 × 300 km; New Caledonia Trough subsided 2–3 km between 55 and 45 Ma [Ö].
- Status: a 2017 team concluded Zealandia meets criteria for a submerged continent, not a microcontinent [Ö].
- New Caledonia highest point (Mont Panié, 1,628 m): BULUNAMADI. Norfolk Ridge rifting age: BULUNAMADI.

### 7.4 Jan Mayen
- Area: 377 km2 (query value): BULUNAMADI. Beerenberg 2,277 m: BULUNAMADI.

## 8. Formation mechanisms, definitions, lifetime, size distribution

### 8.1 Definitions
- Scrutton (1976), "Microcontinents and Their Significance", in Geodynamics: Progress and Prospects (AGU, ed. Drake), pp. 177–189. Primary text NOT read. CONFLICT on scope: one source says Scrutton's narrow definition excludes aseismic continental ridges (e.g. Lomonosov, Jan Mayen Ridge); another says the original term was broad (morphological), with the narrow usage coming later. Needs primary check.
- Durham page on Jan Mayen (search summary): the term "microcontinent" may be inappropriate for large parts of disrupted continental units [Ö].

### 8.2 Formation mechanism
- Müller, Gaina, Roest & Hansen (2001), "A recipe for microcontinent formation", Geology 29(3): 203–206 [Ö, bibliographic and abstract snippets, https://openpolar.no/Record/ftqueensland:oai:eprints.qut.edu.au:234227].
  - Key finding: microcontinents form by rerifting of a young continental margin (<25 Ma old) near a mantle plume stem, followed by asymmetric seafloor spreading.
  - Plume-weakened lithosphere can drive a ridge jump onto the landward weak zone [Ö].
  - Examples named: Seychelles, Jan Mayen, East Tasman Plateau, Gilbert Seamount Complex [Ö].
- Gaina et al. (2003), "Microcontinent formation around Australia", GSA volume on Australian plate: isolation mostly plume-related; East Tasman Rise, Gilbert Seamount Complex, Seychelles, Elan Bank listed [Ö].
- Van den Broek & Gaina (2020), "Microcontinents and Continental Fragments Associated With Subduction Systems", Tectonics 39(8), e2020TC006063 [Ö, abstract only]:
  - Most such pieces are linked to passive margins, but several preserved examples are subduction-related: Coral Sea, South China Sea, central Mediterranean, Scotia Sea, and a proto-microcontinent in Gulf of California.
  - Formation is quick (<50 Myr) and young ages suggest short-lived.
  - Those forming close to large continental masses form faster than those in systems bounded by large oceanic plates.
  - In-situ subduction-related microcontinents: 0.56% of global non-cratonic continental area, 0.28% of crustal volume.
  - Source URL for abstract was not fetchable (doi.org DNS failure); summary from search.

### 8.3 Lifetime
- Subduction-related microcontinents: short-lived (van den Broek & Gaina 2020) [Ö].
- Rerifting of young margins (<25 Ma) produces microcontinents (Müller et al. 2001) [Ö].
- Jan Mayen: ~Eocene breakup (~56–53 Ma) to Oligocene isolation (~24–21.6 Ma) [Ö]: ~30 Myr from breakup to isolation.
- Zealandia: separation 79–83 Ma; submersion by ~23 Ma [Ö]; not classed as microcontinent (2017) [Ö].

### 8.4 Size distribution
- Wilkinson, McElroy & Drummond (2018), "Broken Sheets—On the Numbers and Areas of Tectonic Plates", GSA Today 28(6): plate sizes fit an exponential distribution (R² 0.87 for 52 plates [Bird 2003]; 0.93 for 20 plates [Gurnis et al. 2012]) [Ö]. Continents and calderas reportedly show similar distributions [Ö].
- Microcontinent-specific size distribution: BULUNAMADI in the sources checked. Full-text section of van den Broek & Gaina 2020 not accessed.
